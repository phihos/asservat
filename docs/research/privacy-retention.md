# Privacy and retention feasibility review

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: GDPR-relevant product controls and operational feasibility, not certification or a legal opinion on a particular organization's processing. Primary guidance below was checked; the canonical EUR-Lex English regulation page returned a bot challenge during this review, so legal findings rely on the linked Commission/EDPB guidance. Requirements and defaults below are our engineering choices unless expressly identified as a legal rule.

## Findings from primary sources

| --- | --- | --- |
| Storage limitation | Retention depends on necessity, purpose and applicable legal duties; the Commission does not prescribe a universal debugging retention duration. ## Product gaps found and closed in the design

The earlier design had configurable defaults and expiry checks, but no concrete administrator policy for maximum retention by evidence type/privacy. It measured age from collection completion, did not give derived outputs an unambiguous inherited deadline, and allowed a hold without specifying the ceiling conflict. Those omissions could preserve user evidence longer than intended.

The new [privacy specification](../../specs/privacy.md) introduces `EvidenceRetentionPolicy`, a baseline plus stricter cluster/workload rules, separate privacy/access classifications, and conservative handling of unknown data. `expiresAt` cuts off access before the latest permitted storage deadline to reserve time for deletion. Parentage, cache/backup copies, slow uploads, policy tightening and failed deletion now have explicit semantics and acceptance scenarios. An example sets user evidence to 24 hours maximum and cores/heaps to 6 hours; these are illustrative organization settings.

Existing encryption protects stored evidence but does not make it anonymous. Derived backtraces/profile summaries may retain identifiers, paths or labels. Our inference is to apply potential-personal-data policy by default across the complete evidence catalogue and allow stronger classification from protected workload settings. Redaction checks must not be marketed as proof of anonymization.

## Responsibilities the software cannot replace

The deploying organization determines its role and lawful processing arrangements, including compatibility of diagnostic reuse, applicable special-category conditions, transparency notices, retention rationale, processor contracts and subprocessors, transfer safeguards and whether a DPIA/prior consultation is required. Self-hosted Asservat does not make its maintainers a recipient of production evidence. Hosting, support access or external AI services can change the actual recipient/processor picture; document the deployment rather than assign roles from the product name.

A maximum is an enforced policy with observable failure states, not a physical-deletion guarantee against unavailable disks, malicious infrastructure administrators or storage-enforced locks. We can deny access on time, initiate deletion early, verify registered stores, detect lateness and stop further affected collection. We cannot truthfully certify disk sanitization or erasure from a developer's external AI history. No broad backup exception or silent “grace period” should turn a six-hour maximum into days of retained snapshots.

The remaining consequential product choice is whether an installation may explicitly enable separately reviewed legal retention exceptions. The default is disabled; ordinary holds never bypass the cap. This preference was put to the user. The feature must not claim to adjudicate whether a supplied legal justification is valid.

## Implementation/release evidence still required

EXP-18 must exercise deadlines during upload/analysis, pre-trigger age, parent retention, current/noncurrent versions, replicas, offline spools, early erasure, restricted processing, policy tightening, failed deletion, lock conflicts and restoration. Test with synthetic user markers in cores/logs/profiles/packets. Verify both absence from managed copies and accurate incomplete/overdue reporting when a copy cannot be checked. EXP-10 continues to qualify each actual S3/KMS implementation; AWS documentation is not a Ceph guarantee.

The [full design review](../reviews/design-review.md) adds absolute backup-horizon arithmetic and storage-level index expiry in REQ-PRV-008. A finite backup TTL or hidden expired search result alone does not meet a maximum across every copy. These are engineering controls awaiting EXP-18, not a new legal retention period or compliance certification.
