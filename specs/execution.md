# Accepted execution identities and digest scopes

These contracts separate request provenance, collection approval and sharing. A digest proves identity of a scope, not authority to execute it. The [collection-scope contract](contracts/collection-scope.schema.json) is a resolved DTO written by the hub, never a user-writable Kubernetes resource. Mechanisms follow [ADR-330](../docs/decisions/330-event-driven-system-design.md).

## REQ-EXE-001 — Separate immutable scopes

Store three identities:

1. **Submission digest:** the complete authored spec and its origin: the authenticated hub session for browser/MCP writes, or the source object UID and `metadata.generation` for Kubernetes/GitOps writes (actor attribution comes from `managedFields` and the Kubernetes audit log; no hub-signed submission envelope). It preserves exactly what was submitted, including sharing, resultAccess and pause. Re-applying an identical spec is a no-op; mutating a collection-affecting field needs fresh acceptance.
2. **Collection execution digest:** the resolved collection-scope/v1 record. Include the source cluster, namespace and workload by name (and the workload UID only under `adoption: pinUID`; otherwise UIDs are provenance) and the capture/policy UID, executor identity, resolved team policy authority when applicable, pod selection, observation/evaluation, detectors, action/recipe/preset digests, budgets, effective retention/local ceilings, endpoint/runtime/image constraints and resolved dependencies. Policy generation itself is provenance, not scope: unchanged execution scope retains approval across a label, pause, sharing or grant-reference revision. The source policy UID remains bound. Ad hoc scope binds one capture UID and its requested durations, so a second request cannot reuse its approval. Approval-required plans are marked `timing: approvedAcceptance/v1`: the reviewed scope binds finite action durations, and the capture deadline (the requested recording duration plus the finalization margin) starts when the approval is decided, because the approval itself submits the approved request ([ADR-335](../docs/decisions/335-usable-by-default.md) D5). Accepted deadlines and capture identities never restart on replay.
3. **Sharing scope digest:** the immutable resolved sharing instruction, including exact collection execution digest, creator, management team group IDs, recipients, projections, counts and expiry. Sharing acceptance and raw-sharing approval never authorize collection. Editing only sharing creates a new sharing instruction without forcing a new dump approval; captures already admitted under the old instruction keep it.

For DebugPolicy, `policyAuthority` is mandatory. It records the installation, the delegating AccessBinding by namespace and name, the digest of the binding's resolved automation scope (team groups, allowed actions and ceilings) and rate limits. Its executor is the derived policy principal; the human author remains in submission provenance. DebugCapture forbids this field. The hub evaluates the current binding on its watch events: a change that leaves the resolved scope digest unchanged keeps acceptance; a narrowing or removal stops new starts and stops running sources (REQ-LIF-004); a broadened or otherwise changed scope needs fresh acceptance and applicable review. See [team policy automation](policy-automation.md).

Policy lifetime follows [ADR-335](../docs/decisions/335-usable-by-default.md) D6. A policy whose actions are all non-intrusive (context, logs, pod status, non-intrusive profiles, sanitized network headers, syscall metadata) has no expiry and no run count; per-second rates, cooldowns, ring sizes and storage quotas bound it. Automatic intrusive actions (dumps, commands, intrusive profiles, HTTP-header, raw-packet and syscall-argument recording) need a policy grant that expires after the administrator-configured period (default 90 days). An approver renews that grant in place before or after expiry: renewal sets a new expiry on the same grant, keeps the collection execution digest, generation and counters, and is audited; renewal notices start at `renewalNoticeSeconds` before expiry. Login, re-apply, restart and retry never renew it.

Exclude grantRef, sharing, resultAccess, suspended, free-text reason and idempotencyKey from the collection spec. Their submission/control records remain authoritative separately. A decided approval attaches itself to the policy or capture whose current scope digest it covers; the attachment is audited and cannot modify the frozen plan. Users never copy a grant identifier. Pausing cancels future starts immediately. Resuming the same scope checks current authority, capacity and unexpired approval; it is neither a new approval nor a budget reset. Changing target, executor, recipe, trigger, runtime pack, image restriction, observation scope, limits or effective privacy ceiling requires a newly accepted scope and applicable review. Do not infer safety of arbitrary “narrower” edits. Pause, deny and retention tightening take effect when their event arrives (ADR-330 D1): the hub refuses new starts and pushes a stop for affected running sources over the connector connection, even while reapproval is pending. Content expiry and fixed hard ends are never extended.

Users and GitOps reference recipes, capture profiles and endpoints by name, with an optional version ([ADR-335](../docs/decisions/335-usable-by-default.md) D4); the hub resolves the name, records the resolved digest in `status` and in the collection scope, and an approval binds that resolved digest. Changing the named recipe's content changes the resolved digest and needs fresh acceptance. Resolve referenced configuration before hashing; never hash only mutable names. Normalize before hashing: integer bytes/counts, canonical duration units, UTC timestamps, ordered argv/DAG/sequence lists preserved, set-valued fields sorted by their documented identity with duplicates rejected. Numbers outside the portable exact-integer range or ambiguous normalization fail. Serialize with RFC 8785 (JSON Canonicalization Scheme) and hash with SHA-256. One Go implementation produces every digest; clients never compute hashes.

collectionProvenance.planDigest and SharingIntent.source.planDigest mean this collection execution digest. collector.policyDigest, where present, remains the authored policy provenance digest; it is not the action grant matching key. Keep both names unambiguous in API documentation.

DebugPolicy and other CRDs follow the Kubernetes controller pattern: the hub reconciles on watch events, compiles the current `metadata.generation` into a resolved collection scope and reports `status.observedGeneration` with conditions. A generation whose resolved execution digest is unchanged keeps its acceptance and approval; a changed digest needs fresh acceptance. Observing a generation never by itself accepts execution or revives an approval.

Compilers emit `effectiveRetention`, sorted by action ID, with each action's resolved purpose, privacy class/type, requested age, maximum age and deletion budget. These values participate in execution identity: changed resolved limits cannot reuse approval. Manual recipe and composed captures support the same optional `retention` request as presets. Normalize omission through current defaults and serialize whole seconds. The collector image is acquisition provenance, not collection scope: a collector upgrade does not invalidate approvals. Diagnostic runtime packs and gadget digests remain pinned dependencies.

### SC-EXE-001

Given an approved policy, when its grant is renewed in place or only recipients change, then the collection execution digest stays fixed while submission/sharing identities change. Re-applying the unchanged policy YAML changes nothing. Adding a core, another target or a broader ceiling changes collection scope and invalidates its approval. A manual capture with no policy can request approval for its own immutable capture identity, which cannot authorize another capture.

## REQ-EXE-002 — Acceptance, deadlines and progress

The hub authorizes an operation once: identity, permission, deny, retention, recovery and capacity. It reuses that decision for the operation for at most `identity.AuthorityRecheckInterval` (30 s) and never past an original caller, capture or grant deadline ([ADR-311](../docs/decisions/311-operation-authority-recheck.md)). There are no per-stage, per-helper or per-frame observation windows; five seconds is only an I/O timeout. SQL constraints and conditional updates in the effect transaction enforce capacity, stored retention deadlines and idempotency.

An interactive request has one deadline, at most 15 seconds, carried as its request context. Admission, start and the history write share it; nothing renews it. Stored capture, write and grant deadlines are never extended by a retry.

A DebugCapture written through Kubernetes needs no idempotency key and no absolute deadline: its operation identity is its UID and `metadata.generation`, an optional `reason` is shown to approvers, and its deadline is computed at acceptance from its durations, so a late GitOps sync never applies an already expired request. Browser and MCP requests carry a client idempotency key. Each execution has an operation ID (capture UID, action ID, attempt) stored with its row and inserted with `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING`. The hub sends the start to the region's connector as a stream on the connector's single multiplexed connection (ADR-330 D2); the connector starts the work idempotently under the same ID. A retry, lost reply or hub restart looks the ID up and returns the existing outcome; it never starts the work twice. A process-affecting start whose outcome stays unknown is reported `Indeterminate` and never retried automatically (REQ-ACT-001); a context-only start is resolved by lookup and may be retried under its ID.

The connector has a standing identity (ADR-332) and authenticates to the hub's one HTTPS endpoint with its cluster's projected ServiceAccount token, which the hub checks against that cluster's TokenReview (ADR-334); no Secret is copied between clusters. In-cluster executors authenticate with an audience-bound projected ServiceAccount token checked by TokenReview once per connection. The region trusts its own Kubernetes RBAC and Helm-rendered workloads; no executor Pod, UID or process proof is re-verified at start. Before each attach the executor checks the target identity locally under REQ-TGT-002 (pod UID, runtime container ID, host PID and start time, node boot ID); that check needs no hub round trip.

Preview and request target resolution read cluster state that the connector pushes when it changes (ADR-330 D1); they execute no operation and confer no capture authority. Completed acquisition provenance and ciphertext receipts allow verification and publication after the observed Pod disappears; progress and stored bytes alone authorize nothing. Progress reaches browsers and MCP as events (ADR-330 D1). A transport result is not artifact publication or a successful download. Failures are reported as structured logs and metrics labelled with the failing stage; they carry no identities, credentials or payloads.

Allocate an opaque capture identity at acceptance, before any intrusive approval request. Resolve and freeze the plan, current pod set, per-action fanout/ceilings, privacy and deadline. A manual capture's deadline is its longest requested action duration (a recording's `duration`, default 60 s, administrator maximum 1 h) plus the finalization margin; it is not a fixed five minutes. An approval request waits for the approval window (default 1 h, administrator maximum 24 h); the deadline of the approved actions starts when the approval is decided. The collection-scope/v1 record for DebugCapture MUST contain podBindings: a nonempty list sorted by podUID, unique by podUID and bounded by target.maxPods. Cluster and namespace identity come from source: users name clusters and namespaces, and the hub generates the internal cluster ID from the cluster name ([ADR-335](../docs/decisions/335-usable-by-default.md) D4). A DebugPolicy lives in its workload's namespace of the workload cluster, so its location supplies both. Each binding uses the execution class selected by capability, never a caller option. The node class omits executionClass and requires podUID, nodeUID and nodeBootID; it resolves only scheduled, currently authorized pods. A replacement pod, rescheduling or node boot invalidates that node-bound manual start. The kubernetesAPI class is limited to one builtin-pod-status-v1 snapshot: its binding contains podUID and executionClass, forbids nodeUID/nodeBootID and permits an unscheduled Pod. It binds the exact namespace, workload owner chain and Pod incarnation; replacement still invalidates start, while scheduling the same Pod does not. No missing node identity implies API fallback. The class participates in execution identity and in the verified manifest. Neither class permits an empty approval that fills itself later. DebugPolicy has no fixed podBindings: its authorized observation/selection rules follow current replicas, and each incident pins its admitted concrete pods. These are internal identity fields, not container-level configuration. A matchedProcesses plan binds its logical selector and permits fresh process resolution within those pinned pods immediately before dispatch under REQ-ACT-008; selectedPID binds the exact process from acceptance. Pending approval is a per-action condition. Run independent permitted context within its own reservations; reserve expensive impact/storage only close to dispatch to avoid holding scarce capacity during a human's review. Approval does not reserve indefinite node/storage service. Each process-affecting child start uses the same parent's aggregate bounds and its own operation ID and reservation.

Starting uses the operation's single authorization; before each attach only the local target identity check runs. If the pinned process is gone or the request deadline passed, do not start against a replacement or extend the request. Offer an explicit fresh capture while keeping old context and denial details. Cancellation is idempotent, blocks unstarted work and requests safe cancellation of active work; Indeterminate remains possible. Deleting a CRD is not evidence deletion, nor automatic sharing revocation.

A summary phase is a projection of action states: Running while any action runs, AwaitingApproval only when no runnable action is active and some remain eligible pending review, Finalizing after acquisition ends. Terminate Succeeded only when every required action succeeds; Partial when useful artifacts exist with missing/failed work; Failed when required evidence failed without usable output. Report cancellation and uncertain execution explicitly. No successful artifact is hidden by a waiting optional action.

### SC-EXE-002

Given an accepted API context capture whose start reply is lost, when the hub retries with the same operation ID, then the connector returns the existing execution and no second start occurs. A retry after the capture deadline returns the recorded outcome and starts nothing.

Given cheap context and a required intrusive action, when the approver decides after 40 minutes, then context ran once at acceptance and was visible immediately, and the intrusive action starts at approval with its full requested duration. When the approval window ends undecided, the intrusive start never occurs and the terminal result explains partial evidence. A refreshed page or repeated MCP request returns the same capture without resetting its clock or counters.

## Repeating a retained request

REQ-HIS-001–004 in [capture history](capture-history.md) define restoration from an authorized immutable settings snapshot. Repeat lineage is a non-authorizing source reference; each accepted repeat binds its own capture UID, requester, deadlines, current dependencies and collection digest. A client retry of that operation is still deduplicated. No old grant, sharing instruction or capacity reservation is copied. A missing source snapshot cannot be reconstructed by executing its current namesake recipe. The settings snapshot is written in the acceptance transaction; when the history quota is full the capture is accepted without a snapshot.

## Composed capture identity

For profileComposition, retain authored components/edits, immutable resolved definitions, expanded recipe and action alias map. Include each CaptureProfile as a captureProfile dependency identified by catalog, name, version and definition digest, plus every transitive endpoint/engine/projection dependency; the source object reference is provenance only. The expanded recipe digest is authoritative for actual actions and SharingIntent recipeDigest even without a user recipeRef. Availability is checked at start from the hub's profile cache, which watch events keep current; it is not part of the immutable definition. A disabled or deleted version cannot be started through an earlier preview or old history.

Compilation is deterministic under [capture profiles](capture-profiles.md). Initial combined bounds and inferred values are previewed before acceptance; user edits, profile upgrades or materialized customization create a new collection scope. Captures/policies and repeat requests execute through the existing admission path.

The collection-scope/v1 DTO records composition provenance in its top-level profileComposition field; collection.spec uses the normalized expanded recipeRef with a service-owned `composition-<24 digest characters>` name and the complete expanded recipe digest. That internal name does not require a public EvidenceRecipe object or caller publication rights. The dependency set must exactly cover the resolved profiles. Authored CRDs keep their original profileComposition form.

## Source sessions and incident execution

Normalize each action's executionMode using the immutable contract-version defaults in [action lifecycles](action-lifecycle.md). Persist resolved modes in accepted plans and their digests. Authored recipe/profile digests still identify their exact input; compiled effective-plan identity additionally binds default resolution. Explicit mode changes cannot reuse approval by claiming a harmless scheduling edit. Split ongoing-source activation from the incident graph; dependencies on an ongoing action refer to this incident's output, never to terminating its source.

Service-owned sourceSessionID identifies one authorized recorder and its reservations. Each incident owns separate snapshotID/action attempts and a t0-anchored window. Source lifetime/readiness and incident action timeout/completion are separate state machines. Retrying an incident references its original snapshot and does not attach another source. A manual capture owns a finite source session; cancelling its capture stops that session, whereas cancelling a policy incident stops only that save. No accepted history record reactivates an old source identity.

## Assessment identity

[Capture assessments](capture-assessment.md) bind the resolved execution digest and rule/capability digests. Keep the assessment ID and content-minimized warning basis with acceptance and approval review; a report is not an execution permit. Do not hash changing metrics/timestamps into the collection grant scope or demand policy renewal on a measurement. A material new interactive warning appears in a new preview; changed collection/retention scope follows ordinary acceptance and review. Arming and dispatch rely on admission ceilings and circuit breakers, not on re-running the assessment. Idempotent retries of an accepted operation return that operation, without reacquiring or manufacturing a new consent step.

## Recording-save execution identity

[REQ-SAV-001–004](recording-saves.md) define a separate recording-save/v1 scope rather than disguising a subset as collection-scope/v1 or reusing a full-recipe grant. `recordingSaves.enabled` is collection-affecting policy configuration and participates in policy digests/review. Its omission normalizes to false. Enabling it never retroactively authorizes old source sessions.

The save scope binds exact source/pod/node/boot/action/definition identities, source audiences, requested lookbacks, requester, unchanged policy executor, limits and dependency pins. Sort sources by sourceSessionID, reject duplicates by that ID and by podUID/actionID, and limit selected distinct pods to the policy's incident cap. The canonical save-scope digest becomes collectionProvenance.planDigest and the per-capture sharing source digest. The existing policy execution digest remains separate provenance/authority; it cannot be substituted for the save digest. Acceptance allocates the capture UID and stores t0 and deadline once in the accepted execution row; every idempotent retry returns them unchanged. Only this typed operation may consume a reviewed policy save permit; ordinary ad hoc actions retain individual approval.

Preview operation kinds are service-owned and disjoint. request_capture dispatches a recording-save request only to the snapshot executor and a collection request only to the ordinary executor. Authority, source identity, retained audience, effective privacy/capacity and save enablement are authorized once at submission; a submission whose recomputed plan digest differs from the `expected_plan_digest` is refused with the change. Metadata-only policy revisions do not change the accepted execution digest by themselves. No creator/initiator field widens source evidence access.

A manual group is authorized once (ADR-311) and its children are dispatched concurrently, at most eight at a time, each with its own operation ID. The group's audit row is written in its acceptance transaction (ADR-330 D6). Group StartBy is selected once within the original caller/session, source and processing hard ends; a retry or continuation never extends acquisition authority.

## Result access bound to a capture

[Investigation approval](investigation-approval.md) adds a separate canonical resultAccess digest over `{captureUID, executionDigest, resultAccess}` (the latter excludes its own digest). Canonical serialization follows the scope rules above. The human review scope includes both collection and result-access digests. A result-access edit requires a fresh approval; it neither mutates the collection execution digest nor restarts acquisition. The accepted request protects the authored instruction, and only the authorization component writes resolved scopes/child resultOrigin. A query cannot replace a parent by another capture with an identical recipe. Existing collectionProvenance.planDigest keeps its original meaning.
