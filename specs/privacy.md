# Privacy and administrator retention limits

The initial implementation preserves ordered source changes through [durable retention checkpoints](../docs/architecture/retention-checkpoints.md). Installing current write protection does not authenticate pre-existing policy values. Accept those initial values once against the complete snapshot and exact protection; subsequent guarded changes need no additional human approval. Restart or audit acknowledgement alone MUST NOT assert retained-evidence catch-up or current completeness.

For REQ-PRV-002/004, current-source completeness MUST include durable watch progress through the complete LIST's resource version, not merely matching final objects. A transient added-then-deleted policy remains a reconciliation obligation. Preserve the original finite observation deadline while processing that history; bookmarks MUST NOT renew it. Source availability additionally requires every earlier catch-up and its source/lifecycle audit receipts. [ADR-056](../docs/decisions/056-retention-watch-watermark.md) defines the qualified same-resource ordering profile and no-progress behavior.

The complete source topology is protected installation configuration. A request MUST NOT omit a local source, and a missing source MUST NOT fall back to a hub-only intersection. The initial [topology profile](../docs/architecture/retention-topology.md) requires exact immutable configuration/content pins and an explicit local-source decision. Source identity migration must preserve prior obligations; it is not an ordinary per-capture approval step.

For REQ-PRV-002/004, new collection, publication and disclosure effects MUST revalidate the complete accepted source versions and original finite observation at their final transactional boundary. A source epoch/checkpoint change, omitted source, unaudited catch-up or expired observation MUST prevent new effects. Historical audit/accounting and no-effect replay cannot extend authority. See the [implementation contract](../docs/architecture/retention-effects.md); these internal checks do not introduce another user approval.

Read with [evidence lifecycle](evidence.md), [authorization](authorization.md) and the [privacy research](../docs/research/privacy-retention.md). These are proposed product controls. Their implementation and an operator's processing arrangements must be assessed before making compliance claims.

## REQ-PRV-001 — Purpose, classification and collection minimization

Use “user data” for data concerning or supplied by users of the observed application, including internal tools. This product term is distinct from the legal classification “personal data”; privacy controls also apply to other sensitive application data.

The installation administrator MUST configure an approved diagnostic purpose, permitted workload scope, storage/analysis locality, client disclosure policy and finite retention policy before production collection is enabled. Reuse these settings for ordinary captures; maintainers supply incident context rather than making a legal-basis decision on every request. Store purpose/policy references, not legal documents or user details in CRDs. An authorized operator documents legal basis, special-category conditions where applicable, transparency, processor arrangements and DPIA assessment outside the evidence payload.

Privacy classification is independent of `raw`, `extract` and `metadata` access classes. Use `unknown`, `potentialPersonalData`, `personalData`, `specialCategoryPossible` and `verifiedNonPersonal`. Unknown evidence MUST receive at least potential-personal-data restrictions. Core/heap dumps, logs, arbitrary files, command output, profiles and network captures default to potential personal data; approved workload metadata may raise them to personal or special-category-possible. Profile labels, paths, IP addresses and audit identities can identify people. Encryption, redaction, payload stripping and hashing alone do not establish anonymity.

Collectors derive evidence type from the actual acquisition operation: `coreDump`, `heapDump`, `packetCapture`, `syscallTrace`, `logs`, `files`, `profile`, `commandOutput`, `context` or `other`. A heap delivered through a custom command/file collector remains a heap when known; an arbitrary binary file or command output without a qualified producer classification uses conservative `other` rules, not an asserted filename/type. A maintainer cannot downgrade classification or relabel a core as metadata to escape limits. Only a reviewed, versioned transform with an administrator-approved field model may produce `verifiedNonPersonal`; a scanner finding no matches is insufficient. Derived artifacts inherit parent deadlines even if the projection is verified nonpersonal in this first release.

Default presets collect bounded diagnostic context. Full environment, arbitrary annotations, local-variable values, packet payload, broad recursive files, all-node capture and high-frequency continuous profiling are not default evidence. Redact/project before durable retention where feasible; do not promise complete removal of user data from binary dumps. Operators can forbid classes/actions for selected workloads entirely through collection ceilings. A diagnostic requirement does not authorize collection that the purpose/ceiling excludes.

[Capture assessment](capture-assessment.md) presents conservative possible content before collection, including transient inspection versus retained fields and the actual configured mechanism. Go heap allocation profiles and full heap dumps need different explanations. Notices are mechanism-derived warnings, not scans proving absence, legal consent or a compliance certification.

### SC-PRV-001

Given a core with synthetic user records and a profile with a user-bearing sample label, when collected or transformed, then both receive privacy controls regardless of file extension or extract class. A user-supplied nonpersonal label and a clean pattern scan cannot weaken those controls.

## REQ-PRV-002 — Canonical administrator policy and precedence

For retained evidence, an increased deletion budget MUST fit inside its already-bound hard deadline. A simultaneous increase of the maximum MUST NOT extend that deadline. If no positive readable interval remains, close access and make cleanup immediately eligible; report an insufficient cleanup window rather than inventing extra retention time.

`EvidenceRetentionPolicy` is a namespaced CRD stored in the protected hub control namespace; local administrators may install additional policies in the collector control namespace. Kubernetes API/GitOps is its configuration interface. Only retention administrators may write it, within their delegated scope; source administrators, maintainers, share owners and action approvers cannot raise limits. Admission checks scope and values, `/status` reports observed generation, effective policy digest, backend compatibility, propagation and bounded blockers.

Require one installation-wide baseline with finite maxima for evidence, case metadata and audit metadata. Additional policies can select enrolled clusters, namespaces and workloads by administrator-controlled selectors. All matching policies apply; the shortest maximum wins. Local policies only tighten the hub baseline. Rules can further shorten evidence retention by privacy class and evidence type. There is no priority-based override or user-selected storage/retention profile. Treat unknown classification as matching potential-personal-data rules; a stricter known classification also matches less-specific personal-data rules. Use signed policy snapshots with a default 30-second freshness and a 60-second maximum; connected changes invalidate them, and leases never add that lifetime serially to another permit. Status reports cluster acknowledgements and maximum outstanding lease age, so a Kubernetes write is not claimed as instantaneous fleet activation. If policy/selector provenance or freshness is unknown, do not start collection or disclose affected evidence. Existing objects retain their last bound deadline and remain eligible for deletion.

The baseline supplies requested defaults; narrower policies may lower them. Keep proposed defaults of evidence 72 hours, content-minimized case metadata 7 days and audit metadata 90 days, with the same finite baseline maxima. These are configurable product choices, not GDPR-approved periods. The [example](../examples/retention-policy.yaml) deliberately uses shorter limits: user evidence at most 24 hours and core/heap dumps at most 6 hours. App maintainers can request shorter retention; longer requests are clamped and displayed. Separate collection permission does not override retention.

Within scope, selector dimensions are ANDed; cluster IDs and rule lists are ORed within their own dimension. Omitted scope fields match all within the writer's authorized scope; an empty scope is the installation baseline only in the hub. A rule's present privacy/type dimensions are ANDed; omitted dimensions match all. Privacy matching expands unknown to potential-personal-data, personal to personal/potential, and special-category-possible to special/personal/potential. `other` evidence conservatively matches every type-specific cap in scope until a qualified producer establishes a narrower type. Taking the minimum prevents disguising a core in generic output to gain longer retention. At most 64 applicable policy references are permitted per plan; overflow fails planning with a scope/consolidation diagnostic. Pin these match results, not just mutable names.

When several matching policies provide defaults, use the shortest default. Use the largest required deletion budget inside the shortest applicable maximum; reject an intersection with no positive usable time. Applied policies must agree on the approved purpose reference rather than silently discarding a conflicting purpose. Explicit-recipe and composed manual captures accept the same shorter `retention` request as presets. The current pure resolver implements this intersection; protected Kubernetes matching and fleet propagation remain separate requirements.

Policy updates first produce a bounded impact preview: affected classes, counts, earliest deletions, storage-lock conflicts and incomplete clusters. Applying a shortening through Kubernetes is authorization to enforce it on existing and future matching evidence; no second portal confirmation is required. A longer maximum/default applies only to new collections. It never extends an already assigned deadline or resurrects evidence. Deleting an override never extends old deadlines; deleting the only baseline suspends new collection. Pin namespace/workload UIDs and applicable policy digests at collection so label changes, deletion/recreation or scope reassignment cannot lengthen retained artifacts.

### SC-PRV-002

Given a global 24-hour limit and a local 6-hour core rule, when a maintainer requests 7 days or moves a core into a shared case, then its limit remains 6 hours. Tightening to 2 hours schedules existing older cores for immediate expiry/deletion. Raising to 48 hours does not revive or extend previously bound artifacts.

The initial [retention observation and API admission components](../docs/architecture/retention-observation.md) now implement typed policy reads, protected-fact matching and structural/native write checks. Source acceptance, complete protected topology, remote propagation and retained-evidence shortening remain required before this becomes production retention authority. Status is informational, never a replacement for those checks.

## REQ-PRV-003 — Age, access cutoff and deletion deadline

Age starts at the first evidence acquisition into Asservat, not upload completion, case creation or last access. Include the oldest retained pre-trigger ring segment. For imported runtime files, use an earlier trustworthy source-creation time when available; record `acquisition` versus `sourceCreation` basis and uncertainty. Do not assert knowledge of how long an application retained data before Asservat. A derived artifact uses the earliest parent anchor. Clock uncertainty is resolved conservatively toward earlier expiration; an unsafe clock prevents fresh sensitive collection or disclosure.

Resolve and record the following on each artifact:

```text
hardDeleteBy = min(anchor + every applicable maximum, parent deleteBy values)
expiresAt = min(anchor + requested/default age,
                hardDeleteBy - deletionBudget, parent expiresAt values)
deleteBy = min(hardDeleteBy, expiresAt + deletionBudget)
```

`expiresAt` is the access cutoff and start of deletion; `deleteBy` is the latest permitted storage deadline. Reserve a configurable deletion budget inside the maximum (proposed 5 minutes), not as an extra retention allowance. Reject policy values that cannot leave positive usable time. Thus a 6-hour maximum with a 5-minute deletion budget allows access for at most 5 hours 55 minutes. Show both timestamps and which administrator rule constrained them. Policy `retention` remains a requested access duration, not permission to exceed either deadline.

Reads, shares, cursors, analysis staging, query results, caches and uploads MUST respect these deadlines. Check expiry before starting and before releasing any output; terminate/clean analysis at the cutoff. Do not commit an upload that is already expired. The first acquired byte starts the clock even if a core takes hours to transfer. Each recurrence receives new deadlines for its new evidence and never renews old artifacts. Mixed-source results use the earliest parent deadlines. Case titles/snippets, indexes, manifests and stored query text/results containing evidence-derived personal data inherit that deadline; a content-minimized case shell may follow the case-metadata policy. Audit has its separate finite purpose/retention and never embeds evidence content.

The encrypted manifest records privacy class, evidence type, purpose reference, classification source, anchor/basis, requested age, resolved maximum, deletion budget, `deleteBy` and every applied policy UID/generation/digest. Existing `expiresAt` is the read cutoff. Append signed lifecycle decisions for later tightening; never rewrite immutable provenance. Ordinary policy reconciliation can only reduce the original deadlines. The explicit preservation decisions in REQ-PRV-005 are the sole controlled exception to monotonic shortening: a normal hold may extend requested expiry within its recorded hard cap, while an authorized legal exception replaces only the restricted-preservation storage deadline. Neither silently restores ordinary access. Temporary copies register the same deadlines before data is written; they need not wait for a final manifest.

Present relative effective access/deletion ages before acquisition and current artifact timestamps afterwards. A shorter request can make actual deleteBy earlier than the administrator maximum. Views/sharing/MCP use the same scoped handling notice and current deletion state; automatic deletion covers managed copies, not already disclosed external copies. Do not show verified deletion solely because a timestamp has passed.

### SC-PRV-003

Given a core acquired at 12:00 with a 6-hour cap and 5-minute deletion budget, when upload completes at 12:20 and an extract is produced at 17:50, then neither can be read at 17:55 and both are due for deletion by 18:00. Sharing, case recurrence, copying or analysis cannot restart the clock. A pre-trigger ring starts its deadline at its oldest included acquisition time.

## REQ-PRV-004 — Delete every managed copy and report failures

Maintain an encrypted lineage/copy inventory for artifacts, derived extracts, query output, catalog/index/cache material, object versions, replicas, staging volumes, node spools and multipart parts. Collectors and analysis workers enforce local expiry leases during hub outages. A retention worker independently deletes all known copies/versions and verifies backend acknowledgements/version inventory, removing wrapped per-object keys and sensitive manifests as part of cleanup. A delete marker alone is not deletion. No per-copy or completed-case CRD is required; SQL holds content-minimized copy references, lineage and active hub lifecycle work; full inventory/deletion outcomes remain encrypted in object storage, with bounded local work in Kubernetes.

Lifecycle rules provide a backstop; they do not prove deadline deletion. Distinguish `Accessible`, `ExpiredDeletionPending`, `DeletedVerified` and `DeletionOverdue`, with last attempt, affected storage class, next retry and opaque copy references. `DeletedVerified` means verified removal from all registered application-managed stores, not proof of physical disk sanitization or third-party erasure. Missing/offline node acknowledgements and inaccessible replicas block that status. Retain a minimal, independently expiring tombstone sufficient for restore suppression; do not retain filenames/user identifiers as a deletion receipt.

Retry safely with bounded backoff, alert on `deleteBy` overrun and expose oldest overdue age. An overrun is a policy breach requiring response, not an automatic extension of the maximum. By default pause new collection into the affected allocation when deletion is overdue; use a storage-profile-wide pause only when inventory cannot establish narrower isolation or the failure affects that backend. Unaffected backed allocations remain usable under [capacity isolation](capacity.md). A storage outage, offline machine or immutable object can make physical deletion late: the product MUST report this limitation and never certify an absolute physical-deletion guarantee. Access remains denied throughout. No shared Transit-key deletion is described as selective cryptographic erasure.

Bounded cleanup pages MUST advance past failed attempts and revisit them in finite scheduling passes; a permanently failing oldest page must not hide later due work. Scheduling cursors are not authority and never extend retention, renew deletion leases or drop pending audit obligations.

Exclude raw evidence, derived evidence and plaintext scratch from general-purpose backups by default. Qualified backup/replication profiles must honor each absolute deletion deadline across all copies; a fixed snapshot lifetime that extends an artifact beyond its cap is incompatible. Control-state backups omit evidence content, have finite retention and restore behind an authorization gate. Before serving any restored data, apply current ceilings, expired grants/shares, lifecycle decisions and tombstones from the authoritative journal; unknown state fails closed. Image/symbol caches may have a separate policy only when they contain no captured user data.

Cleanup must first fence future writes and confirm that all in-flight writers, uploads and multipart completions for the obligation have stopped. A worker lease expiring or an empty inventory alone is insufficient: a late writer could recreate data after a false deletion acknowledgement. Unknown quiescence retains the charge and pending/overdue status. The initial [custody implementation](../docs/architecture/custody-transactions.md) makes this a mandatory port. The bounded API context path now has [positive writer completion and independently audited cleanup](../docs/architecture/context-deletion.md): all four writes must be acknowledged and the one-use producer joined before its immutable completion can authorize purge. This protocol does not establish quiescence for failed or uncertain writes, other collectors, arbitrary storage credentials or restored state. Complete runtime supervision and finite metadata/audit retirement remain pending.

The initial [public-operation audit lifecycle](../docs/architecture/operation-audit-lifecycle.md) similarly separates positive write closure, original-object purge, finite deletion proof and terminal absence verification. Removing identifying live rows does not immediately free capacity still backing recoverable SQL history. Keep that capacity through the qualified horizon, including a residual pool floor after the final cooling aggregate is deleted. Actual overdue retirement may delay safe capacity reuse; it never shifts original retention dates or establishes compliance with a missed deadline. Its currently bounded healthy/on-time path is not general outage, unknown-writer or restart recovery support.

The explicitly admitted [overdue-original branch](../docs/architecture/operation-audit-overdue.md) removes positively closed original audit objects after an outage before any certificate claim. It permanently fences certificate creation and preserves the original finite cleanup budget and all retention dates. Purge need not wait for the independent absence verifier to become available; unavailable verification retains capacity charges rather than retaining otherwise safely erasable objects. A separate current acknowledgement remains mandatory before release. The missed proof window is recorded without an identifying permanent tombstone. Unknown writers, old records and recovery are not covered by this branch.

### SC-PRV-004

Given an expired object with a noncurrent version, a derived result and an offline node spool, when deletion runs, then a successful current-object DELETE does not report global completion. Access is denied, pending copies remain visible to operators and deadline overrun alerts. Restoring an old catalog cannot disclose or recreate expired evidence.

## REQ-PRV-005 — Preservation and immutable-storage conflicts

An ordinary debugging hold may extend a shorter requested lifetime only up to the administrator's already applicable maximum, and never grants readership. It requires explicit retention authority, scope, reason, finite expiry and audit. It cannot bypass erasure/restriction decisions or renew evidence after expiry. Retention holds are service-managed decisions on existing evidence, not permission to create another uncontrolled copy.

Default `legalExceptions: disabled`: even a hold labeled legal cannot exceed the maximum. The optional `reviewed` mode is a proposed choice pending the user's legal-hold preference. If enabled by the installation administrator, a separate legal retention decision must identify exact artifacts, a protected legal-case reference, authorized compliance reviewer distinct from requester, finite replacement deadline, review date and applicable justification. All matching policy authorities must permit the exception, including local ceilings. Record original and exceptional deadlines; surface the exception in compliance inventory. It authorizes restricted preservation, not ordinary debugging/MCP access beyond original expiry. No automatic renewal or blanket namespace exception. Missing review/expiry fails closed for processing; release/expiry resumes deletion under the controlling decision. A legal-reference string alone never validates a legal basis.

Separate mutable evidence retention from audit integrity policy. Do not enable blanket Object Lock/legal hold on the evidence bucket by default. Preflight checks bucket defaults, each object's lock mode, replica locks and backup retention before accepting a storage profile. A storage minimum longer than the permitted maximum is `RetentionConflict` and blocks new affected collection. A compliance-mode lock can make shortening impossible; deny access, report the conflict and escalate to the retention administrator, never claim it was deleted. Changing policy cannot retroactively remove an unbreakable lock. Storage-enforced preservation is advertised only for qualified backend modes; application holds are labeled accordingly.

### SC-PRV-005

Given a 6-hour maximum and an ordinary hold for 3 days, when requested, then it cannot extend storage beyond 6 hours. A 30-day compliance-mode bucket fails preflight. If a conflicting locked object already exists, shortening the policy revokes ordinary access and reports the unresolved storage conflict without fabricating a deletion receipt.

## REQ-PRV-006 — Rights requests and restricted processing

Provide an authorized privacy-operator workflow to locate candidate evidence by workload, time, purpose and controlled incident references; inspect lineage; restrict processing; request early deletion; and track completion/exceptions. Do not create a permanent global user-content index merely to support search. Optional application-supplied opaque subject references are encrypted, access controlled and have the evidence deadline; they are hints, not proof that untagged cores contain no subject data.

Restriction prevents ordinary analysis, disclosure, sharing and reuse while an authorized preservation/rights workflow resolves the request. A lawful preservation decision can retain data without enabling ordinary investigation. Early erasure propagates to all derivatives and copies through the same reconciler, subject to a separately reviewed applicable exception. Access/correction workflows prepare bounded, reviewed personal-data extracts for the controller's established response channel, protect other people's data, and record decisions. They do not expose an entire raw core or add a public evidence-download endpoint. Immutable evidence is not silently edited; annotate disputed facts or generate a corrected derived view with provenance, deleting originals when required.

Restriction state MUST be durable, explicitly initialized and lineage-aware. A mutable policy object's empty list, a newly published matching capture or a replacement artifact ID cannot establish unrestricted processing. The bounded context mechanism in [ADR-127](../docs/decisions/127-current-processing-and-download-placement.md) uses immutable artifact/workload-time scopes and separately audited exact revision releases. It reserves release audit capacity before admitting a restriction; normal traffic cannot consume it, and rule toggles/retries cannot reset uncertain spend. Business rows, initialization attempts and their backup/WAL histories require independent finite backing and qualified retirement; migration or configured deadlines do not prove cleanup. Unsupported derivatives and unknown restored processing state deny.

Automated content discovery cannot prove complete coverage in arbitrary cores. Report uncertainty and permit a conservative broader deletion/restriction by workload/time. Export a content-minimized processing/inventory report: purposes, locations, categories, retention, sharing/disclosure recipients, processors configured by the operator, deletion backlog and exceptions. Do not claim that the product independently fulfills every data-subject request or determines its legal exceptions.

### SC-PRV-006

Given a validated erasure request covering an incident, when a privacy operator deletes its candidate artifacts, then derived summaries, versions and query caches enter the same deletion plan. A legally preserved item is separately restricted and explained; lack of a user tag is not reported as proof of absence.

## REQ-PRV-007 — External agents, transfers and operational privacy

Apply locality policy to raw storage, decryption, derived results, hub indexes, backups, support bundles and configured event destinations. The hub region and authorized remote client disclosures are part of the data-flow inventory. Local raw storage alone does not establish that all personal data remains in that region. The administrator approves clients/data classes for external AI disclosure and supplies the relevant organizational handling policy. Authentication/client allowlisting constrains recipients, but cannot establish or enforce their eventual model provider, geography, logging or retention.

Use bounded extracts and least disclosure by default. Do not log prompts/tool results or send product telemetry. Asservat expiries govern Asservat-managed copies; results already delivered to users/agents cannot be recalled. Record the registered recipient client and disclosure time for investigation, without storing raw query output in audit. Operators arrange downstream handling, contracts and applicable transfer safeguards; no server-side enforcement claim substitutes for these obligations.

Document breach response with an auditable incident timeline, involved evidence classes/recipients, containment/revocation and deletion status. A capture trigger is not automatically a personal-data breach. The operator assesses notification duties and deadlines. No automatic regulator/user notification or external message is sent by the product.

### SC-PRV-007

Given an approved agent receives a bounded extract, when the original expires, then Asservat denies further access and deletes managed copies while reporting prior disclosure. It never claims deletion from that agent's history. A raw-locality profile that sends personal extracts to another region exposes that flow in the inventory and policy preview.

## PostgreSQL, protocol evidence and retained copies

REQ-PRV-004 includes PostgreSQL base backups/WAL, replicas and restored databases as managed control-data copies. Keep evidence bytes, full manifests and extracted text out of SQL. Minimal identities/relationships still have finite metadata and backup retention. Row deletion is not backup erasure; REQ-DB-005 supplies restore gating and old-authority fencing. Regional text indexes and HTTP header projections remain user-data evidence with inherited first-acquisition deadlines. Live rings, parser buffers, frozen segments and optional encrypted disk spools have separate bounded copy inventories/expiry. Neither network headers nor an HTTP allowlist establishes anonymous data. Team management or result sharing never extends an artifact's cutoff or grants an external AI client permission beyond its policy.

## REQ-PRV-008 — Backup horizons and expiring index segments

Finite backup TTL is not enough: deleting a row on day 90 and retaining that backup for seven more days stores it for 97 days. For each content-minimized control-data class define first-purpose anchor, absolute hardDeleteBy, and the qualified maximum residual backup/WAL/replica horizon H. Remove identifying live data no later than hardDeleteBy - H - cleanupBudget; expire every backup/WAL chain containing it by hardDeleteBy. Include overlapping base backups, replication slots, snapshots and restored copies. A profile whose live-use/recovery dependencies cannot fit that arithmetic is incompatible and must be corrected before collection. Current configuration has its separate active administrative purpose; historical copies cannot gain a new anchor through backup. Keep only minimal non-identifying replay/fence records where necessary, with documented finite horizons; unresolved authority/accounting dependencies block a false deletion claim.

Regional evidence-text indexes MUST have deletion at the storage-copy level. Initial implementation uses bounded memory indexes on qualified no-swap/no-core nodes, with full teardown/rebuild by the earliest indexed deadline; no general backup of evidence text. An optional durable index profile partitions by finite expiry cohorts, encrypts each independently and deletes the entire cohort, snapshots and obsolete segments by the earliest member deadline. Maximum cohort duration defaults to five minutes and may shorten availability; query filtering or a Bleve document-delete alone is insufficient. Tightening/early erasure invalidates and destroys affected cohorts, then rebuilds only eligible records. Cap shard/file count and rebuild work, and report IndexRebuilding while structured SQL catalog discovery remains available. Do not claim disk sanitization or shared-key selective erasure.

### SC-PRV-008

Given 90-day metadata and a seven-day residual backup horizon, when a backup profile is planned, then live removal is due by day 83 minus cleanup margin. A day-90 row DELETE is rejected as insufficient. Given an index segment containing a six-hour core extract, expiry/early deletion removes the managed segment copies, not only its search hit.

## Discovery and recommendation copies

The [guided selection](guidance.md) acquisition recipes have an initial 15-minute requested lifetime and ordinary classification, approved projection, deletion budget and hard retention clamping. Samples, process/flow projections, analysis scratch, cached pages and evidence-bearing recommendation drafts are managed copies. Do not copy paths, IPs, packet-derived text or user goals into unbounded SQL/audit fields. The five-minute navigation-handle lifetime is not a fresh retention anchor. Saving a selector as recurring configuration is explicit and does not retain sampled traffic/process lists or extend their expiry.

## Capture settings retained for reuse

REQ-HIS-003 in [capture history](capture-history.md) governs history summaries and parameter snapshots independently of artifact retention. A seven-day summary default does not authorize keeping evidence-derived settings, arbitrary user identifiers or arguments for seven days. Content-bearing snapshots use encrypted regional records and the applicable shorter deadline; only approved pure configuration may use the finite metadata policy. Repeating neither renews source content nor revives expired evidence. Apply backup/WAL horizon checks to minimal history rows and normal managed-copy deletion to snapshots. UI/MCP report summary availability separately from repeatable-settings availability.

## Reusable configuration and syscall traces

`syscallTrace` is an evidence type subject to all administrator maxima, acquisition-time deadlines, derivatives and managed-copy deletion. Both metadata and arguments default to potentially personal; argument/buffer variants never obtain a longer retention window by reclassification as a profile. Approved metadata projections exclude arguments, paths, pointers and buffers before release.

CaptureProfile definitions are configuration, not an exemption for captured data: no credentials or discovery samples may be laundered into indefinitely retained Kubernetes specs, catalog descriptions or SQL indexes. Parameter edits, composition previews and history snapshots retain evidence-derived lineage/deadlines where applicable. Exporting/saving a reusable profile requires the configuration privacy admission checks in [capture profiles](capture-profiles.md); profile versioning does not reset source retention.

## Transient live-view privacy

Live viewing is processing of potential user data even without a saved artifact. [REQ-LIV-005](live-watch.md#req-liv-005--privacy-transient-data-and-saving) applies source audience/authority, region, field minimization and inherited deadlines before emission. No generic raw live content is supported in the first release. Per-row expiry is at most 30 seconds and no later than source/content/view authority; server replay is at most five seconds. Browser rows are cleared on known access loss, logout, closure and expiry, including paused selection. Paused visible views poll bounded status to learn revocation; disconnected browsers rely on local deadlines. Payloads never enter SQL, outbox, logs, URL parameters, browser storage or backups. Protected scope/filter objects have their own short deletion ledger. External agents may copy delivered text; notices must not promise recall or automatic deletion outside managed components.

Authorized native downloads are supported for all evidence under [REQ-DLD-004](artifact-downloads.md). They do not reset managed retention or create a remotely erasable laptop copy. Record the disclosure, deny new/resumed transfers after revocation/expiry, and label external-copy handling clearly. This investigation feature is distinct from scoped privacy-rights responses.

The initial [retained-evidence lifecycle components](../docs/architecture/retention-lifecycle.md) implement separate monotonic read/deletion cutoffs and signed encrypted lifecycle audit for accepted pod-status context. Restrictive SQL changes take effect before journal availability; journal failure never preserves old readability. Source-policy acceptance, remote propagation and verified physical cleanup remain mandatory production prerequisites.

Retained policy resolution must preserve the policy relationships recorded both at collection and by later shortening decisions. Follow their protected policy UIDs after label changes, and intersect newly applicable rules; missing/deleted overrides never renew old dates. Processing an ordered lower-then-higher configuration transition must apply the lowering to affected retained evidence before treating the source as current. A final matching LIST value alone cannot establish that no intervening maximum existed.
