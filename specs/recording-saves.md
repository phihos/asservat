# Save existing recordings now

This is a first-release, snapshot-only invocation of a policy's already authorized ongoing packet/syscall sources. It does not evaluate a fake detector, rerun the recipe, start a source, change filters or collect a dump. Use the existing capture service, SQL execution/capacity/audit state and regional publication pipeline; no new CRD or service is needed for the save operation. The protected [recording-save contract](contracts/recording-save.schema.json) distinguishes it from ordinary collection.

## REQ-SAV-001 — Discover eligible recordings and explicit policy scope

`DebugPolicy.spec.recordingSaves.enabled` authorizes eligible workload maintainers to request bounded saves from that policy. Omitted means false. It is part of the collection execution digest and policy grant review: enabling it requires normal acceptance and any applicable fresh approval. Policy authoring with ongoing profiles offers it enabled in the visible authored draft; existing or hand-authored policies are never silently changed. The reviewer sees that maintainers may save existing recordings even without a detector firing. A policy with no ongoing actions cannot enable it.

The caller needs current whole-pod collection entitlement for every selected pod and the configured client scope. The policy execution identity must independently retain all original acquisition/save authority and applicable sensitive observation grants. Under ADR-059 this is the recorded team policy identity and delegation, not continued membership of its creator. The save initiator still requires fresh personal authority. No creator-token refresh or migration of the source executor occurs. A policy's authorization is never transferred to the caller: the save runs as that policy's narrowly approved snapshot operation and retains an immutable initiating actor separately. A share, evidence-reader role, grant handle or opaque recording handle alone confers no collection ability. Disablement, local deny, suspension, source authority expiry/revocation or policy execution-digest change invalidates new saves, including accepted but unstarted work. Current source leases bound disconnected behavior. Exact namespace/workload/pod UIDs protect name reuse; stopped pods require current entitlement to their retained workload scope, never the new namesake.

Expose **Save existing recordings now** in the authorized policy/workload view. Passive inventory shows only permitted policy/pod/action rows, source state, observed history duration/gaps, configured maximum lookback and eligibility/blocker. No packet contents, syscall arguments, process inventory or hidden-source counts. Handles are opaque, principal/client-bound, short-lived (at most 60 seconds), tied to exact source session/node boot/action/filter and policy execution identity; they are not bearer authority. Reading inventory/preview does not read payload, pin buffers, reserve capacity, extend leases or arm collectors. Paginate at 20 rows by default, maximum 100; expire/re-authorize cursors normally.

### SC-SAV-001

Given a maintainer and an extract-only share recipient, when they inspect one workload, then only the maintainer can discover/request eligible saves. A false-to-true recordingSaves edit changes reviewed collection scope. A still-running source under the old scope, a disabled policy or a replaced namesake cannot be saved through an old handle.

## REQ-SAV-002 — Snapshot identity, window and narrow execution

One save selects 1–16 distinct source handles from one policy execution scope, cluster, namespace and workload. Apply the policy's incident pod cap and local ceilings; this selection may choose any authorized observed pod within that cap instead of needing a triggeringPod. UI defaults to the currently selected pod and its eligible sources, and displays any cap before submission. Do not silently drop unauthorized selections or combine sources from different policies. Only ongoing packetCapture/syscallTrace actions are eligible. A source with zero configured history cannot supply a historical save; show NoHistoryConfigured and an explicit separate future-capture choice.

Default lookback is each selected source's policy-configured before. An optional common before can only shorten those bounds and must be positive. This operation has **after: 0s**: it saves already observed bytes, without extending recording into a new post-window. Future capture remains an explicit ordinary capture choice. Preserve representation, filters, endpoint/action identity, source audience, limits and privacy ceiling exactly. No new dependencies, context actions, runtime profiles, cores, commands, process selection or acquisition parameters are accepted.

The passive preview allocates a capture identity and freezes a protected recording-save/v1 scope: requester, original policy executor/reference/execution digest, exact source/pod/node/boot/action/definition identities, per-source lookback/output caps, pod cap and effective ceiling/retention pins. Its canonical digest binds the preview and capture assessment. The preview expires within 60 seconds; no capacity or history is promised during that interval. Metadata-only policy generation changes are provenance; execution identity and save enablement remain authoritative.

On first successful submission acceptance, persist one immutable t0=acceptedAt with its clock uncertainty, original finalize deadline, scope digest and idempotency binding. This is the end of every selected source's requested [t0-before,t0] interval. The caller cannot supply t0 or a future window. t0 belongs to the signed accepted execution in addition to the scope digest; neither retry nor dispatch may change it. Node receipt later than t0 may lose history; it never captures newer bytes to compensate. Clock uncertainty, retained-source grace, local coverage and earliest content expiry bound usable evidence. Use a qualified wall-to-monotonic mapping and exclude records that cannot be placed before t0 within its bound; unknown mapping produces ClockUncertain/partial or refusal, not a claim of exact coverage. Warming/degraded/stopped-but-still-valid sources may yield explicit partial output; a different source epoch never fills the gap.

Grouped dispatch requires each child's own exact acknowledged intent before that child starts; it does not wait for unrelated sibling acknowledgements. Optional encrypted history follows mandatory start attempts. The prospective browser/MCP request_capture composition window is 15 seconds, bounded by the original caller and identity/session validity; admission facts retain their original five-second interval and accepted snapshots retain StartBy=t0+5s. Independent child outcomes remain visible after admission. A fresh accepted-only response-metadata read never changes t0, StartBy or execution authority. See ADR-268.


After admission, each source save is independent. Race with rotation, pod/source loss or revocation produces Partial/Failed/Denied under normal capture state rules. At least some meaningful retained coverage must exist to save; a fully observed quiet interval can be a valid empty-record artifact with coverage metadata. A wholly absent source yields no fabricated successful evidence. Already validly frozen snapshots can finalize under their remaining processing/storage authority. Cancellation releases only this capture's snapshot references/reservations and never stops its separately authorized live recorder. No source restart or unapproved fallback occurs.

### SC-SAV-002

Given a policy containing packets, syscalls and a live core, when the user saves two sources with 20 seconds of lookback, then only packet/syscall snapshots are admitted. No core or new recorder starts. A stale source after preview is rejected or reported lost after acceptance; retry retains the original capture/t0. A retired source can provide only the still-authorized portion ending at its recorded stop.

## REQ-SAV-003 — Budgets, privacy, audience and sharing

Manual saves use the same policy/workload occurrence cooldown/rate, concurrent snapshot/output, per-node, protected storage and fair upload limits as automatic incidents. The accepted policy authority's `maxExecutions` counter charges once per Save, regardless of selected source count (maintainer decision, 2026-10-30). Applicable grant execution counters remain per source as specified below. The false/true detector hold/rearm condition is deliberately not needed for this separately authorized operation, but is not reset by it. No reserved emergency bypass. Bursts of clicks or multiple users/policies remain subject to shared workload accounts. Offer an already admitted capture only when the caller may read it, as a clearly labeled alternative rather than a new save. Refuse with a precise blocker/retry time when immediate bounded admission is unavailable; do not silently queue a fresh future save after cooldown. Duplicate delivery of one accepted idempotency key returns that capture. A different request with the same key conflicts. Cancellation cannot refund ambiguous starts or reset counters.

Relevant policy grant save/action budgets are reserved as well: one sensitive snapshot start per source plus worst-case output bytes where the grant requires them. Preserve cumulative observation spend; saving existing bytes does not count a second wire observation or spend a live-core execution. Ordinary snapshots still consume capacity/occurrence budgets. Pin/copy/encrypt only after the required local reservations, authority and durable audited permit are ready. No per-click human approval is needed when this exact manual-save capability was already approved; missing approval blocks the save and links to policy review rather than retaining unapproved buffers while waiting.

The assessment describes the **incremental** freeze/copy/encryption/upload cost and continuing recorder baseline separately. Saving creates no new probe/ptrace pause, but collector CPU/memory, storage and transfer may still contend with the workload. Preserve unknown overhead, possible sensitive contents, source-age retention and external-agent handling warnings. The UI may preview and submit in one interaction when its bounded selection and disclosure are current; a material scope/warning change refreshes that review. A preview never guarantees how much history survives until admission.

Retention starts at original source acquisition, not the save click or upload. Inherit the earliest applicable deadlines, processing restrictions and retained audiences of included segments, intersected with current rules. Split outputs at incompatible audience boundaries or use the intersection; never union old and new ownership into wider access. New creator/initiator status, policy operation and current exec rights do not grant raw read permission or retroactive audience membership. Default evidence access uses these source audiences and current evidence entitlements. Raw queries retain their per-principal/client grants.

Policy recurring SharingIntents do not automatically apply to these manual saves. An owner may attach a new per-capture `sharing` instruction to preview using the existing capture-time declaration/approval/count/expiry rules. Validate the initial instruction before acceptance; pin its source to this recording-save digest and captured action IDs. Materialize only authorized actual outputs and approved derivatives. Pending raw-sharing approval does not freeze a live ring indefinitely or authorize a raw read. Report collection/sharing progress independently.

### SC-SAV-003

Given two maintainers repeatedly save a sensitive ring during an automatic incident, when shared quota/cooldown is exhausted, then later saves are refused without another ring, dump or capacity slice. An admitted save retains the earliest source deadlines/audience. A new team member does not gain old evidence through the click, and a policy's unrelated future-sharing instruction publishes nothing from this save. Explicit authorized capture-time sharing follows its normal checks.

## REQ-SAV-004 — Browser, MCP, history and provenance

MCP uses `list_recordings(target_ref, policy_uid?, cursor?, limit?)`, then `preview_recording_save(recording_handles, before?, reason?, sharing?, repeated_from_capture_id?)`, and the existing `request_capture(preview_id, idempotency_key)`. The last tool remains disabled by default and needs evidence:capture; preview/inventory require current collection eligibility as well. Preview returns operation=recordingSave, a scope digest, selected rows/window, incremental capture assessment, expiration and blockers. Handles are service-owned; users do not enter hashes/session IDs. The browser uses the same services and evaluator. get_capture/cancel_capture retain the existing capture/progress contract with an explicit recordingSave operation discriminator. Refuse supplying a recording-save preview through an ordinary recipe compiler or vice versa. These are runtime capture operations; Kubernetes remains the only policy/permission configuration API.

Grouped progress retains one public parent `capture_id` and 1–16 action rows. Each grouped row has an immutable `action_instance_id` (the actual child identity), retained `pod_uid`/`pod_name`, and the configured `action_id`; the configured action may repeat across Pods. Child identities distinguish results without creating child navigation or submission targets. A parent cancellation requests all unfinished snapshots; stored results win and remain available. `cancellation_requested` is not proof that remote work has joined. Per-source capture-assessment rows use `recording_handle` to bind the existing selected row, retain its original source acquisition retention anchor, and describe only the incremental save cost; they do not relabel a packet snapshot as pod status or claim measured ongoing-recorder overhead.

Accepted saves appear in the same selector-scoped history with origin=manual and operation=recordingSave. A bounded protected recordingSaveSettings record retains only the target, policy UID, action IDs, lookback preferences and original scope provenance, subject to the normal audience/settings deadline. Do not include the policy's other recipe actions, source handles, grants, sharing instructions or live leases in repeat parameters. Repeat opens **fresh source selection** via current inventory and preview, retaining optional repeated_from lineage; never replay the old source/epoch or silently turn this into a new recording/core capture. get_capture_settings returns the distinct typed record. Ordinary preview_capture(history_capture_id) redirects this kind with RecordingSelectionRequired and an authorized navigation hint rather than interpreting it as a recipe.

Artifacts carry ongoing acquisition provenance with triggerKind=recordingSave, t0, original source session, snapshot ID and truthful prehistory/whole-window coverage. Protected recordingSaveOrigin binds the policy UID/execution digest; collectionProvenance.planDigest is this save's recording-save/v1 digest, not the policy's collection-scope digest. Store requester/actor separately from unchanged policy executor; neither is editable by a client. The encrypted detailed artifact manifest retains the exact accepted source-window scope and original source binding, including the original node/boot/collector process identities, with independent digest checks; it remains inspectable after source metadata retirement. Full source/selection manifests and saved settings remain encrypted under the existing privacy ledger; SQL stores content-minimized IDs/digests/limits and work status.

The optional reason is 1–512 Unicode scalars, preserved verbatim and encrypted as descriptive control metadata. It is not justification, consent, collection/grant authority or a scope change. Store no plaintext reason or plaintext reason hash in SQL. Bind one immutable reason reference to the reviewed preview and original initiator; grouped saves retain one parent reason across all children. `get_capture` may display it once after ordinary current metadata authorization for the entire selected group. Unavailable or retired reason text does not change collection/progress authority or extend retention. Repeat never restores the old reason. Its body follows the existing encrypted audit/preview retirement lifecycle (ADR-267); no separate reason service or timer is required.

### SC-SAV-004

Given an enabled MCP client, when it saves an eligible ring and retries the same submit after an ambiguous response, then it gets one capture and can poll/cancel with existing tools. History labels it as a recording save; repeat asks for current recordings and cannot invoke the policy's liveCore action. The evidence manifest identifies both the saved source and the manual-save origin. A request with a dump parameter, mixed-policy handles, after>0, forged executor or reused stale handle fails before new acquisition.

## Saving from a live view

The [live watcher](live-watch.md) may offer **Save current recordings** for a policy source, but must obtain fresh eligible recording handles and use the unchanged save preview/acceptance checks. The selected display predicate is not a capture filter; it cannot narrow/widen source acquisition parameters or define the saved artifact representation. A paused view does not pin a row. Explain that t0 is the new save acceptance time and earlier displayed rows may already have rotated out. Do not attach a live-only source/session to this policy-save API or silently substitute the ongoing policy's recipe. Finite manual-session sources lead to their own capture outcomes. Ordinary owner sharing/retention checks apply only to committed results.

## Immediate save interaction (REQ-SAV-001/002)

Offer “Save last 30 seconds now” (or the actual narrower approved default) directly on eligible source rows/live source context. Display source, available history, output/access/expiry and bounds before the click. An explicit click performs fresh passive preview and admission without another human review page when the accepted parameters and material disclosures are unchanged. “Customize” opens the existing selector/review. If scope or impact changes, block/refresh with a precise explanation; never silently broaden or pin before admission. Browser/MCP callers retain the same preview/request contracts and idempotency. t0 remains the first successful acceptance, so network latency/eviction can still produce explicit missing coverage. The receipt identifies the actual saved source/window; never advertise recovery of evicted history.
