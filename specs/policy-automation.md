# Team policy automation

Accepted ADR-059 refines REQ-AUT-001/003, REQ-PER-001/006/007/008 and REQ-EXE-001. It separates the current rights of people from finite recurring execution. The [research](../docs/research/background-identity.md) records the standards and alternatives. Mechanisms follow [ADR-330](../docs/decisions/330-event-driven-system-design.md).

## Operator configuration and low-effort authoring

`AccessBinding.spec.automation` is an optional reusable **per-policy** ceiling. It is allowed only on an Allow binding whose subjects are stable groups and whose roles include `workload-maintainer`. It uses the binding's own namespace and workload scope and optional `notAfter`. Creating or changing it requires `bind` on `accessroles.asservat.io` named `automation` in that namespace, which only an administrator grants (the chart's non-aggregated automation ClusterRole, [ADR-329](../docs/decisions/329-accessbindings-in-application-namespaces.md)). Namespace writers who may grant roles therefore cannot create an automation envelope.

The [typed envelope](contracts/policy-authority.schema.json) fixes allowed action kinds, maximum policy lifetime in seconds (1–31536000), maximum executions (1–10000), aggregate collected bytes (1–1 TiB), observed bytes (0–1 TiB) and optional cumulative observed events (0–1,000,000,000). An omitted event ceiling means zero and permits no argument observation. Zero observed bytes permits no continuing acquisition; it does not turn an ongoing recorder into an on-trigger action. These are prospective ceilings, not throughput or overhead estimates. Node/workload limits, observation ceilings, storage and grant rules still apply; several policies cannot multiply a workload's aggregate allowance. Action kinds are restricted to the expanded recipe's exact kinds and never waive intrusive review or local safety rules.

See [an administrator binding](../examples/access-binding-automation.yaml). An operator normally configures one envelope per team and workload. A maintainer with current personal rights selects a workload and recipe; a sole matching envelope is chosen automatically, several require a visible choice, and none produces `DelegationRequired` with a Kubernetes/GitOps proposal while keeping the draft. Human-only exec inheritance is not an envelope: remembered groups are never copied into a machine review, and a maintainer cannot name a powerful service account.

## Identity and acceptance

The authenticated creator/submitter, the policy execution principal and the authenticated collector are distinct. The first is audit provenance, the second identifies the bounded policy, the third authenticates dispatch ([ADR-330](../docs/decisions/330-event-driven-system-design.md) D3). The policy principal is `issuer=urn:asservat:installation:<installation UUID>`, `subject=policy:<policy UID>`, `clientID=policy-executor`, `identityType=workload`. This issuer is internal and never accepted by public endpoints; the strings are not credentials.

At acceptance the submitting human must have whole-pod collection entitlement and belong to a sponsoring group of the envelope; the first acceptance fixes the immutable creator and later editors are recorded as submitters. The resolved scope must fit both the human's authority and the envelope. The acceptance records the binding UID and generation and 1–8 sponsoring group IDs (issuer-scoped) that hold `workload-maintainer` through that binding. Sponsors are execution authority, distinct from evidence-owner management; they never come from review sources or catalog ownership. The collection scope records installation, policy UID, workload UID, action kinds, issue and expiry times and cumulative limits. No caller supplies an executor, digest or sponsor by writing CRD status.

`collection-scope/v1.policyAuthority` is required for a DebugPolicy scope and forbidden for DebugCapture. Ongoing sources, trigger incidents and existing-recording saves use the same policy identity. Authored policies contain configuration intent; resolution belongs to acceptance. An optional reference may disambiguate envelopes and is resolved before hashing. No user-computed digest is needed.

Changing executor, sponsors, envelope, expiry or limits changes the execution scope and requires acceptance and applicable review. Login, reapplied unchanged YAML, retries and restarts cannot reset counters or extend authority. Human-bound policy scopes require explicit new acceptance.

## Hub-held policies and activation

A DebugPolicy lives in the hub control namespace and names its workload cluster in `spec.target.cluster`; its target is bound by the accepted authority alone ([ADR-228](../docs/decisions/228-restart-triggered-policy-captures.md)). The authenticated human activates the current generation with `activate_policy` (MCP and browser): hub-api reads the policy, compiles it, resolves the envelope and the human's sponsoring groups, takes the envelope's maxima as limits and accepts it in one transaction with its audit row. A generation is accepted at most once; a retry with the same idempotency key returns the original acceptance. A generation written through Kubernetes is accepted under [REQ-PER-007](permissions.md#req-per-007--submission-and-policy-execution-identity). Captures run under the policy executor; `get_policy_status` explains the policy without content, using `observedGeneration` and conditions.

`deactivate_policy` ([ADR-229](../docs/decisions/229-policy-deactivation-and-console.md)) stops an accepted policy. It requires a current, undenied `workload-maintainer` allow of a sponsoring group, not the creator identity; administrators stop policies through Kubernetes (suspend or delete the DebugPolicy, or remove the envelope). Deactivation clears the acceptance and signals the scheduler in the same transaction (`NOTIFY`); the hub pushes a stop to the policy's running sources and refuses later admissions. It refunds no spend, deletes no evidence, is idempotent and writes an audit row. Reactivation needs a changed generation.

## Continued execution and revocation

The hub watches DebugPolicies with an informer. Suspension, deletion or a new generation is an event that stops new admissions for the old acceptance at once; pause/resume and grant attachment are control fields that never reset authority, and reverting YAML cannot revive an earlier acceptance.

Each source start (arming an ongoing recorder, a trigger dispatch, a save) is admitted once, under one permission snapshot ([REQ-PER-006](permissions.md#req-per-006--freshness-composition-and-explainability)): the accepted scope, the current envelope, every sponsoring group's current allow for that target, denies, grants and budgets. The admission is bounded by every applicable hard end (policy expiry, envelope `notAfter`, grant, local ceiling, session length) and the started source runs to it, as a started download does ([ADR-312](../docs/decisions/312-started-downloads-run-to-original-bounds.md)). The scheduler maintains all authorized replicas within source ceilings by reacting to Pod events, not by a timer. Revocation reaches running sources as a pushed stop: envelope removal, a sponsoring group losing its allow, a deny of the executor or a sponsoring group, deactivation and policy deletion. A connector without a hub connection stops sources after the installation's offline grace period. An unrelated personal deny does not revoke team execution. Additional evidence-owner managers lend no execution authority.

Creator logout, token expiry, departure or a disabled account does not revoke team-policy authority. The creator loses dependent personal actions at once on a local deny, or within the identity freshness bound after refreshed external membership. Deleting an IdP group alone is not a local revocation; operators remove its envelope through Kubernetes. Irreversible effects already performed cannot be undone.

## Approval, evidence and reuse

A policy AccessGrant keeps requester and reviewer provenance and separately binds `policyExecutor` and `policyAuthority`. Self-approval compares the reviewer with the human requester, never with the executor. Effective scope and lifetime are the intersection of envelope and review. Automation identities cannot review. Personal manual collection plus result access cannot be transferred to recurring automation.

Policy-grant metadata needs current authorization: `get_grant` and the requested list require current whole-workload collection rights on the approved workload; a former requester without them sees nothing. Current approvers keep review access. A current member of a sponsoring group may inspect the policy configuration, original and effective dates and grant reservations, so team management survives requester departure; this grants no decision authority, evidence access or personal continuation. `can_revoke` reflects current reviewer or sponsor eligibility, not requester provenance. Requester matching uses issuer, subject, client and principal kind.

The policy principal supplies no human download, raw-query, live-view or sharing rights. Evidence keeps its recorded audience and source deadlines; neither creator nor executor is added to it. Viewers and recording-save initiators are authorized separately from the source's policy. A creator's departure can leave collection running while their personal sharing instruction is suspended; team-managed sharing follows REQ-SHR-008. Both outcomes are visible.

## User-facing explanation and acceptance cases

The policy editor and review show the team, finite expiry, cumulative budgets and continuation after creator departure. Configuration preview and source discovery share the typed execution-authority summary, which has no authorization value. MCP `list_recordings` and policy-owned `list_live_sources` expose the same bounded fields as the browser. Manual sources cannot claim team automation. A missing, expired or removed envelope explains the remedy without asking developers for SSO credentials.

SC-PER-007 and SC-EXE-001 include: creator leaves but the team policy stays eligible; the former creator cannot read or manage; envelope removal, sponsor loss, expiry and scoped denies stop running sources and refuse new admissions; repeated requests cannot reset spend; user-only exec cannot create an automation principal; namespace writers without `bind` on `automation` cannot add or remove an envelope; source and viewer authorization diverge correctly; every sponsoring group must still hold its allow; approval rejects requester self-review; wrong policy, installation or workload and expiry are negative cases.

Personal offline-user execution and SCIM/CAEP integrations remain optional future modes. Browser session refresh is for interactive human access, never a credential kept for recurring jobs.

`get_policy_status` reports the accepted policy's shared execution, native-output and observed-byte counters with their accepted ceilings and expiry. These pools are separate from each grant's reservations and stay shared across grant replacement, pause/resume and sharing controls. Status confers no acquisition, evidence or personal continuation rights.

`request_policy_grant` takes the policy name and UID, finite limits, optional observation limits and period, and an idempotency key. The server resolves policy, acceptance and compiled review; requester, authority, scope, Pod, capture and personal result access are never accepted from the body. It returns the grant detail and review link. A different current `action-approver` decides through the normal review with real step-up. A replay with the same key returns the original result and needs current metadata rights, not a new step-up. The review reason `policyCollection` identifies this approval and is distinct from sensitive-action reasons.

`activate_policy` with `grant_id` attaches a grant to an accepted policy as a current-sponsor control. It never re-accepts collection intent, renews expiry or resets spend; replay does not restore a pointer cleared by a later change. The change and its audit row commit in one transaction. `get_policy_status` reports the accepted collection kind and the attached grant.

Finite onTrigger/before-zero network-header recording uses the triggering Pod and the admitted occurrence key through the recorder preview/request pipeline. Preview eligibility is not admission: admission matches the actual action to the accepted authority and charges the grant and policy budgets. The policy principal never opens a personal review. Recording settings use the accepted issuer namespace without re-authenticating the creator; missing or changed settings refuse new work. Sensitive observation is admitted and settled under [REQ-AUT-006](authorization.md#req-aut-006--sensitive-observation-sessions).
