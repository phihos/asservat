# Trigger storms, workload impact and capacity isolation

Read with [triggers](triggers.md), [actions](actions.md), [privacy](privacy.md) and the [feasibility notes](../docs/research/capacity-isolation.md). These requirements add resource protection independently of permissions: an approved action can still be refused for lack of safe capacity.

## REQ-CAP-001 — Workload-wide storm control

Observe signals continuously within sensor budgets, but admit evidence collection separately. Apply all relevant limits across policy, workload, namespace, node, cluster and storage pool; the tightest limit wins. The workload account includes every selected pod and its runtime sources, manual capture, policy, trigger source and policy generation. Creating another policy, changing a signature, restarting a pod or rolling a Deployment does not reset its counters. Unowned pods/direct selectors remain charged to a namespace envelope and a controller-assigned account; clients cannot choose an account to escape limits. A workload account is keyed by cluster, namespace and workload name (ADR-335 D4); UIDs remain provenance. A recreated workload of the same name keeps its account, so replacement cannot shed retained charges or bypass parent limits.

Keep existing context-capture cooldowns and hysteresis. Add a stricter dump bucket: proposed defaults are burst 1, minimum 15 minutes between dump episodes, at most 2 individual process starts and 4 GiB of uncompressed input reservations in any rolling hour per workload. These are configurable starting budgets, not universal safe values. The interval/burst limits gate a dump episode (one immutable action batch for one occurrence); rolling-hour starts/bytes count every individual process. maxProcessesPerEpisode defaults to one and may be raised through an administrator budget profile for explicit multi-process recipes. An episode freezes its process set and reserves all starts before dispatch; it cannot append reloads/workers or remain open beyond its original action deadline. This allows bounded serial multi-process snapshots without treating unrelated triggers as the same episode. The initial contract keeps episode burst fixed at one and configures the interval/hourly ceilings. Classify kernel cores, live cores, heap dumps and dump-producing diagnostics by their actual operation. Unclassified custom acquisition receives the conservative expensive/dump envelope unless an administrator-qualified recipe proves a narrower class. Reject relabeling to bypass limits. A dump start whose outcome is unknown keeps its start charge, because the process may have been paused, until the collector reports a terminal outcome for that start ID or the node's boot ID changes; its byte reservation is released when the outcome is known. A failed dump does not trigger an immediate retry loop.

Coalesce related signals into the existing occurrence and aggregate further suppression by workload, policy/source and reason in bounded 30-second windows. Preserve first/last times, saturating counts and gaps, not raw matched text or one CRD/object/webhook per hit. Write suppression counters once per window, not per hit. Report why collection was skipped and when it can next be admitted. New signatures may influence selection within the budget; they cannot bypass it. No queued dump is automatically started merely because cooldown expired: the original request must still be fresh and its process identity valid. An ongoing condition needs an explicitly enabled bounded resampling policy to collect again. The ADR-228 restart trigger keeps one suppression row per policy and reason for the policy's lifetime (no 30-second window): its occurrences are already admission-bounded, so the row count is fixed and only first/last times and the saturating count change.

### SC-CAP-001

Given 10,000 matches/minute across 20 replicas and three policies for one Deployment, when all request a core, then workload-wide admission permits only the available dump budget, records bounded suppression summaries and never creates a queue of 10,000 future dumps. Pod restart, policy edit and a different signature cannot refill the bucket.

## REQ-CAP-002 — Admit before causing impact

Before an expensive action begins, reserve authorization, a workload start/byte budget, workload/node concurrency, memory/scratch, storage bytes/object count and an upload service slot. The immutable operation binds every reservation and the resolved action limits. A raw-query grant or policy approval cannot override any resource ceiling. Reject or skip unavailable optional actions before pausing the application; preserve independently admitted cheap context. Do not generate a core first and discover afterward that storage is full.

Default expensive concurrency is one per workload and one per target process, intersected with the node's configured limit (proposed two expensive actions/node). Concurrent captures per cluster default to the larger of 8 and the cluster's node count (`capacity.capturesPerCluster`, ADR-335 D8); the number of policies and sessions is not capped beyond Kubernetes object limits and rate limits. Profiles, commands and dumps share this expensive-work concurrency envelope unless a qualified recipe has a lower class; conflicts inside one runtime serialize as before. Queued requests are bounded (proposed one expensive request/workload, ten/node), expire after at most 30 seconds or their earlier request/target deadline, and carry no unbounded retained data. Expired triggers produce a gap, not a dump of whichever process now occupies that PID.

Natural kernel cores cannot wait in this queue. Spend from the connector's local budget lease under an unexpired local policy permit with immediate node admission, or reject the stream promptly. Each admitted core carries its crash ID; the connector reports usage by crash ID, so a repeated report charges once. The host helper must not contact a remote service while holding the crashed process open. It must not drain a rejected core just to compute a signature. `core_pipe_limit` is a qualified node-wide last-resort bound, not per-workload fairness; helper invocation/kernel work can still occur during a crash storm. Disable Asservat acquisition when local readiness is lost and report missed cores. This platform cannot prevent crashes or all kernel coredump overhead caused by the application itself.

Use an action-specific watchdog and safe detach/stop procedure for live diagnostics. Cancellation cannot guarantee reversal of a command, synchronous kernel I/O, or work started inside an application by HTTP profiling. Do not repeatedly force-stop a healthy process to satisfy a debugging policy. A timeout with uncertain completion remains Indeterminate.

The [assessment panel](capture-assessment.md) explains expected effects, limits and uncertainty before admission. Forecasts do not reserve capacity or weaken a ceiling. Aggregate only compatible scoped quantities, distinguish declared buffers from total memory and show shared pressure without revealing other teams' plans. Current local admission and circuit breakers remain authoritative when a preview becomes stale.

Admission reads the current SQL capacity accounts, which the pool and budget controller keeps up to date from watch events (REQ-CAP-005). There is no readiness wait, refresh demand or projection expiry; an account whose source is unavailable refuses admission with `CapacityUnavailable`.

### SC-CAP-002

Given no available storage/scratch reservation, when a live dump is requested, then no ptrace stop, application heap request or custom dump command begins. A natural core without local budget is rejected promptly. A retry with the same operation ID after a lost acknowledgment returns the existing outcome; it neither starts a second dump nor releases a reservation whose outcome is unknown.

## REQ-CAP-003 — Fair scheduling and workload health

Bound collector, compression, encryption, upload, analyzer and host-helper resources independently. CPU/memory limits on the collector do not bound work performed inside the target runtime, kernel, or a helper outside its cgroup. Qualify helper/worker cgroup placement, memory/scratch limits and cleanup. Prefer low-cost compression settings with a CPU budget; never depend on an optimistic compression ratio for capacity admission. Do not compensate for a throttled uploader by buffering unlimited plaintext or extending process-stop time.

Key-provider load is bounded operations, not evidence bytes: [envelope encryption](evidence.md#req-evd-001--encryption-and-integrity) keeps payloads off that service. Admission MUST bound provider request rate, concurrency, wait time and retry work across replicas, preserving capacity for existing reads. Charge each artifact attempt's key wraps to its workload. A saturated provider delays or suppresses new acquisition within the request deadline rather than creating an unbounded retry queue. Natural-core readiness requires already wrapped keys and local budget; the crash helper never waits for a remote key service. Never fall back to plaintext or reuse a different attempt's keys. Combined provider burst capacity is a release qualification.

Schedule uploads by workload using bounded byte quanta and weighted deficit round robin; never one global FIFO dominated by the first noisy Deployment. Impose workload, node and storage-profile byte-rate/burst and request-rate limits. The connector holds each workload's token bucket and grants nodes bulk token leases of a few seconds of budget, so spreading across nodes does not multiply the workload limit. A restarted uploader forfeits its unused tokens; nothing is persisted. Charge retransmitted bytes and retries to transfer budgets. Use a fair bounded queue for analysis/acquisition as well. Keep a small protected context/control service class so large dumps cannot consume every worker, buffer or I/O request. Fairness does not promise a real-time deadline on a saturated or failed network.

Administrator resource profiles define effective upload service and finite CPU/memory/scratch caps. Preflight checks that the maximum artifact can plausibly fit the acquisition/stop/deletion deadlines at the currently admitted service share. For example, 2 GiB at 32 MiB/s already needs 64 seconds without compression or setup; a 30-second streaming plan cannot promise a complete dump. Report the mismatch and suggest an explicitly smaller dump, longer permitted deadline or pre-reserved encrypted local spool. Never automatically increase caps, process-stop time or workload resources. Local spools have separate per-workload/node reservations and privacy deadlines; an upload backlog is not unlimited disk space.

Add a circuit breaker for new expensive work when collector/node pressure, scratch headroom, transfer backlog, deletion backlog or runtime-specific memory-headroom checks fail. Trip on events, not samples: Linux PSI triggers (`/proc/pressure/*` thresholds delivered by `poll()`), cgroup `memory.events`, scratch write errors and backlog counters crossing their limits; acute allocation/space failures trip immediately. Recover after 60 seconds without a new trip event (hysteresis). Thresholds are administrator profiles tested per node/runtime. Already paused work uses its safe cancellation/watchdog path. Lower-cost context may continue within its protected budget. A failed health probe alone is not a breaker, since it may be the intended trigger. Admit a heap diagnostic during low memory only when its runtime profile's additional headroom requirement is satisfied; otherwise preserve lower-impact evidence and explain the skipped heap.

### SC-CAP-003

Given workload A continuously requests large cores while B requests one capture, when both are authorized and fit capacity, then A cannot consume B's scheduler allocation or protected context workers. A full spool, insufficient heap headroom or unsafe transfer deadline prevents the expensive start. Restarting an uploader or node collector does not refill the workload's token bucket.

## REQ-CAP-004 — Protected storage plus hard ceilings

An administrator configures an `EvidenceStoragePool` over a qualified storage profile, with finite accounted capacity, object count, system reserve, safety headroom and shared burst allowance. An `EvidenceBudgetPolicy` supplies protected bytes/object slots per eligible workload, hard maxima, context reserve within that protected allocation, storm limits and upload/queue settings. Both are administrator-managed Kubernetes CRDs, not a new HTTP administration API.

The chart renders one default pool and one default budget policy from a few operator-facing values (ADR-335 D2): `capacity.evidenceStorage` (default: the bundled object store's volume size; required for an external store), `capacity.workloadShare` (each workload's protected allocation, a quantity or a percentage of the pool, default 2 %), `capacity.workloadLimit` (the per-workload hard maximum, default 10 %), `capacity.capturesPerCluster` and the retention values. The controller derives the system reserve and safety headroom (5 % each), the context reserve, object slots and the other figures from these inputs; byte, WAL, row and footprint figures are internal estimates, not values. Sizes accept Kubernetes quantities. The default policy covers every namespace except system namespaces, the release namespace and namespaces labelled `asservat.io/evidence: disabled`; access still requires an AccessBinding. Changes to these values apply on `helm upgrade` as ordinary policy edits. Administrators add further pools and policies as CRDs for different storage or teams. Local collection ceilings can tighten limits. Workload maintainers inherit the applicable profile and may narrow it; they cannot raise it or select another charge account.

Each enrolled workload uses the matching budget policy with the highest `spec.priority`; ties are broken by policy name. Overlapping matches are reported as a warning condition on the affected policies. No match gives `CapacityNotAllocated`. Changing a selector never creates a second account or detaches retained charges. Namespace envelopes apply to all workloads in that namespace; pool allocation slots bound the total across every namespace matched by the profile. Node limits are intersected with the local administrator's collector-wide ceiling, not independently multiplied for each policy/workload. [ADR-246](../docs/decisions/246-node-collector-role.md): the collector-wide ceilings are the collector's Helm values for source-ring memory, frozen-snapshot memory awaiting upload, and upload rate/burst per node (defaults 256 MiB, 256 MiB, 32 MiB/s, 64 MiB); a budget policy's optional `node.maxRingBytes` and `node.maxFrozenBytes` (at most 4 GiB) and its `node.upload` can only lower them. A reservation that does not fit fails without partial effect, and refused upload never consumes the bucket.

Burst is charged only against the pool's separate burst slice, so admitting burst never consumes a child's unused protected allocation. A namespace, cluster or pool limit lowered below its already allocated protected total reports over-limit and blocks new allocations; it never retracts existing ones.

Protected capacity is allocated when a workload is enrolled for collection, not on its first crash. It is **not lent** to another workload. Shared burst comes only from an explicitly separate pool slice and is best effort, bounded by workload maxima. Allocation must satisfy:

```text
sum(workload protected allocations) + shared burst allowance
  + system reserve + safety headroom <= configured usable pool capacity
```

Apply the equivalent invariant to object slots. Workload charges include committed bytes plus outstanding maximum reservations. Usage within its allocation consumes its own protected capacity; usage above it consumes only shared burst. The allocator reserves those portions atomically and checks the workload hard limit before admission. A single workload may consume its permitted share of burst capacity, but cannot consume another's unused protected allocation. Context reserve is included in a workload's protected allocation and is unavailable to dump/heap/payload actions. System reserve covers bounded catalog/audit/deletion work and is not writable through evidence requests.

Preserve context capacity at the hard ceiling too: expensive evidence must fit `hardLimit - contextReserve`, alongside any already occupied non-context bytes/object slots. Unused context capacity is never lent to expensive actions. Allocation moves or pool resizing cannot retract an allocation with committed or reserved usage; drain it or provide replacement backed capacity first. Slot size and profile protected values must agree before the allocation is Ready.

Context and packet records keep their finite record and object ceilings. Within them, the context reserve keeps the administrator-authored bytes up to the supported packet ciphertext reservation per record; it is not reduced to pod-status size.

Example: a 128 GiB accounted pool allocates 16 GiB safety headroom, 8 GiB system capacity, four protected workload allocations of 16 GiB and 40 GiB shared burst. A workload ceiling of 32 GiB allows A to use its 16 GiB and up to 16 GiB of burst. B, C and D retain their protected capacity even if A has triggered continuously. Each workload can use 1 GiB of its 16 GiB for context that its dumps cannot occupy. Object-count accounting protects against many small artifacts as well as a few large ones.

The guarantee is admission within a workload's **remaining** protected capacity, provided the backing storage is healthy and provisioned. It is not unlimited history or an unconditional new core after that workload fills its own allocation. A logical prefix or S3 bucket does not reserve physical disk. Qualification requires an administrator-assigned backend capacity envelope, replication/erasure-coding overhead accounted by the operator, monitoring of free capacity and protection from unrelated writers (dedicated capacity or a backend-enforced equivalent). Shared physical storage without such a boundary is explicitly `BestEffortCapacity` and cannot satisfy the isolation release claim.

### SC-CAP-004

Given the 128 GiB example with A at its 32 GiB hard limit, when B requests evidence within its unused allocation, then storage admission succeeds independent of A's trigger rate. A is refused before acquisition and cannot evict B's evidence. More protected allocations than the pool can back are rejected at enrollment, rather than silently overcommitted.

## REQ-CAP-005 — Durable hub and local accounting

PostgreSQL holds the authoritative quota accounts and reservations. A controller watches `EvidenceStoragePool` and `EvidenceBudgetPolicy` and applies each change to `capacity_account` rows (pool, cluster, namespace, workload), reporting `status.observedGeneration` and conditions, as the Kubernetes ResourceQuota controller does. Accounts stay valid until the next change replaces them. Namespace and cluster envelopes and the pool invariant (REQ-CAP-004) are checked when allocations change: at enrollment, resize or policy edit.

Each reservation is keyed by its operation and attempt ID and binds byte, object, scratch and transfer quantities. Admission within a workload's protected allocation is one conditional update of that workload's row. Only usage that spills into shared burst also updates the pool row, which is locked before the workload row. Grant approval and capacity remain distinct checks; a hub transaction commits the reservations and work together under [persistence](persistence.md). No database write occurs per packet, log line or byte. Admission never reads an asynchronously lagging replica.

Accounts are ordinary rows updated from the current pool and policy objects on every change, including the Helm-rendered defaults: a raised limit admits more at once, and a lowered one blocks new admissions above it without invalidating existing evidence.

Approved context captures, queries and downloads charge the ordinary workload account. Query and download read work is additionally bounded by per-principal and per-workload token-bucket rate limits and concurrency caps.

The hub grants each connector a budget lease (bytes, object slots, starts, expiry) over its connection and charges the leased amount to the hub accounts. The connector keeps the lease in memory plus one small local checkpoint object written on change; natural cores and disconnected work spend from it. On reconnect the connector reports its usage and receives a new lease; the hub then releases the unused remainder.

Before upload, reserve worst-case stored ciphertext size, compression expansion, manifest/index overhead, multipart/retry allowance, required replicas and bounded planned derivatives; reserve object slots as well. Separately cap uncompressed input, transfer bytes and local scratch: they are different resources. No unknown-size/unbounded reservation. Use a qualified format/backend bound, not expected compressibility. If a necessary derivative cannot be reserved, report partial analysis rather than bypassing quotas. Converting a reservation to committed usage must never double-charge or temporarily release occupied bytes.

Object keys are deterministic: `…/<workloadAccountID>/<operationID>/<attemptID>/…`. A retry with the same IDs writes or HEADs the same key, so it cannot create a second charged copy. An attempt with an unknown outcome is resolved by listing its prefix (objects, versions and multipart uploads) and its charge is released when the outcome is known. Buckets carry an `AbortIncompleteMultipartUpload` lifecycle rule. A new attempt uses a new attempt ID and reserves separately.

Committed objects, noncurrent versions, retained copies and deletion-pending artifacts remain charged until the object store confirms their deletion; expiring a manifest or inserting a delete marker does not return capacity. Successful deletion releases the charge idempotently. Shares reference the original bytes without duplicate storage charge; newly materialized results charge the source workload, with an explicit bounded split for multi-workload results. All required workload accounts must reserve the split, so a comparison cannot charge another team by choosing their baseline. After a database restore, committed usage per workload is recomputed from the bucket inventory of that workload's prefixes (REQ-DB-005).

For the context profile, the retention configuration MUST NOT allow a deletion date beyond the processing-history horizon. The horizon is validated when retention or processing configuration changes; a shorter horizon is rejected or reported as a condition on the configuration. Preview and admission read the stored validation result without locking the configuration and refuse while it reports a gap. A preview reserves nothing and promises no future publication.

### SC-CAP-005

Given two 8 GiB attempts racing for 8 GiB remaining capacity, when both reserve, then only one becomes Ready. A crash after multipart start keeps the charge until listing the attempt's prefix resolves it; the lifecycle rule removes the incomplete upload. A failed DELETE or hidden version does not free space for another reservation. A retry with the same operation ID reuses its reservation and cannot double-charge.

## REQ-CAP-006 — Exhaustion, onboarding and visibility

Default full-account behavior is `RejectNewExpensive`: retain prior evidence, continue only context that still fits its reserve, and report `WorkloadStorageFull`/`PoolBurstFull`/`CapacityUnavailable` as appropriate. A fair scheduler must still admit other workloads' available protected capacity. Do not pause unrelated accounts merely because A reached its cap. Scope a deletion-related profile pause as narrowly as reliable inventory permits; a backend-wide deletion failure or physical pressure still affects the shared backend.

An administrator may enable `DeleteOldestEligibleInAccount`: delete only the same workload's oldest unheld eligible evidence and derivatives, explain the shorter effective availability, verify removal, then reconsider a still-fresh request. Never evict another workload, override preservation, wait indefinitely while a process is stopped or assume deletion has completed. Retention is a maximum, not a promise to keep every artifact until then; the default avoids unrequested eviction. Holds continue consuming capacity and require preview of the account impact. The pending legal-exception preference is unchanged.

Apply namespace/cluster allocation envelopes and account-count limits so creating many workloads cannot monopolize enrollment or evade parent budgets. Automatic onboarding allocates from an administrator's already assigned namespace capacity without a per-app ticket; if it cannot supply the selected protected profile, report `CapacityNotAllocated` before calling the policy Ready. Workload deletion/name reuse does not erase charges. Release its protected allocation only after retained charges are safely accounted in a backed residual account or all copies are deleted. Raising an allocation or moving retained evidence requires checked backing capacity; lowering below usage blocks new admissions and reports over-limit rather than deleting other workloads' data.

Show maintainer-friendly usage: retained, reserved/in-flight, deletion-pending, protected remaining, burst use, object slots, upload pressure, suppression count and next eligible start. Every skipped action names the limiting scope and remedy without exposing another team's inventory. Defaults come from the chart's default budget policy (REQ-CAP-004); ordinary users and operators need no quota YAML. Administrative views show overcommit prevention, orphan reservations, physical/backend pressure and allocation readiness. MCP/case results distinguish missing evidence because of resource limits from evidence that never existed.

### SC-CAP-006

Given A fills its account, when another trigger arrives, then it records suppression and available context without deleting B's evidence. If own-account rolling deletion is enabled but an object is held or deletion fails, its charge remains and no dump starts. A newly enrolled workload without backed capacity is visibly pending, not falsely protected.

## Discovery shares the same limits

[Guided surveys](guidance.md) are ordinary admitted capture episodes, charged to the same workload/node/storage/transfer accounts. Surveys use the existing workload rate budget; its survey defaults are one concurrent survey per workload, a 30-second start interval and ten starts per hour across users and clients. They cannot consume protected context reserve by masquerading as free previews. Existing incident capture takes priority; wait/refuse visibly when budgets conflict. Passive recommendation reads never reserve acquisition capacity, but retain bounded query/cache quotas.

## Composed collectors and syscall event pressure

Reserve the whole expanded composition under one workload/capture account. Context deduplication removes only identical independent snapshot acquisitions; packet/syscall rings, frozen copies, parser/thread maps and observation budgets remain separate reservations. Concurrent policies/captures contribute to the same workload totals. Qualified coexistence or a feasible serial schedule is required for exclusive profilers and process-stopping actions; a duplicate expensive acquisition is a preview conflict. A profile may not use a separate namespace/catalog to obtain a second protected workload allocation.

Syscall event/rate/thread caps bound acquisition work separately from output bytes. Argument reads consume cumulative grant events and bytes before processing; dropped/error-filtered events and ring rotation cannot refund observations. Loss/overflow and impact circuit breakers are visible under [syscalls](syscalls.md). These specifications do not yet demonstrate acceptable production overhead.

## Admission at source activation and at trigger

[Execution modes](action-lifecycle.md) split capacity admission. Reserve steady ring/parser/CPU/event resources for every armed observation pod before any source attach. At an admitted trigger, separately reserve frozen segments plus replacement writable memory, output/object slots, encryption/spool/upload and onTrigger impact for the selected incident pods. Copy-on-write is not free memory; bytes pinned by multiple snapshots remain accounted until references settle. Coalescing cannot extend an accepted window indefinitely. Source/session lifetimes and per-incident action deadlines have separate finite limits. No-trigger recordings still consume observation budgets; upload congestion cannot turn a bounded source into unlimited retained storage.

## Saving existing recordings

Recording saves spend the same policy/workload occurrence cooldown/rate and per-source snapshot/encryption/storage/upload reservations as automatic saves. Multiple initiators and request paths cannot allocate independent budgets. Existing observation charges stay with their source; only incremental frozen/output resources are added, and no dump allowance is consumed without a dump. Sensitive save/action counts remain under the applicable reviewed policy grant. Refuse unavailable immediate admission with a bounded reason/retry time; do not queue a new t0 after cooldown. A snapshot whose outcome is unknown keeps its charge until the outcome for its snapshot ID is known. Cancel only its snapshot references; keep an independently authorized live recorder running.

## Live-view isolation

[REQ-LIV-004](live-watch.md#req-liv-004--protect-collection-and-workload-resources) defines independent viewer slots, scan/CPU, replay memory and output/transfer counters within the existing workload account rows. A filter that returns few rows can still scan many events; enforce both. Charge retries and preserve counters through edits/pause/reconnect. Source rings, frozen snapshots and protected incident uploads are separate reservations; no per-view ring pin. Apply workload and principal caps across tabs/clients, with node/installation aggregate limits. Slow clients drop/stop rather than block acquisition; retain explicit unknown/lost coverage. Operator metrics/capacity views report counts/bytes/reason enums without live content or high-cardinality row labels.

### Ongoing source resource limits

The node collector's Helm values set its installation-owned ceilings for `maxActiveSources`, `inputBytesPerSecond` and `inputBurstBytes`: complete positive values or all zero (zero denies ongoing sources). The collector watches `EvidenceBudgetPolicy` and applies, on each event, `effective = min(node ceiling, policy node/workload limit)`. Zero or absent policy fields lower nothing; they never multiply a node or workload allowance by Pod, policy generation or incident `maxPods`. Sources receive shared slices whose sums fit the aggregate ceilings, never one full allowance per source. Policy automation `maxObservedBytes` is a separate limit: zero cumulative observed credit denies acquisition, and ordinary sanitized sources do not acquire a raw grant to fund these limits.

Development reference defaults are sixteen active sources, 16 MiB/s aggregate input and a 16 MiB burst per node. Structural validation ceilings (1,024 sources and 10 GiB/s/10 GiB) are parser/arithmetic bounds, not qualified profiles.

The collector request of 20m and CPU limit of 500m bound its user-space cgroup, not kernel TC/BPF execution. Source count/rate, small-packet CPU, kernel maps/perf buffers and combined live/frozen memory require native performance qualification before ongoing collection is enabled. These development defaults establish no per-source CPU cost, safe throughput or approval step.
