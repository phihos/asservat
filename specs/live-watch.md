# Live observation of ongoing captures

Live watch is a first-release view of already-running packet and syscall sources in the browser and MCP. It complements durable evidence and [recording saves](recording-saves.md). Watching never starts a collector, widens acquisition or pins a recording window. The [wire contract](contracts/live-watch.schema.json), [research](../docs/research/live-watch.md) and [mockups](../docs/ux/README.md) define the boundary. The initial design exposes approved packet/flow, HTTP-header and syscall-metadata projections; raw bodies, packet bytes, memory and syscall arguments require saved artifacts and existing raw-query review. Mechanisms follow [ADR-330](../docs/decisions/330-event-driven-system-design.md).

## REQ-LIV-001 — Eligible sources and current authority

The passive source inventory `list_recordings` (shared with [recording saves](recording-saves.md)) takes the closed named `target_ref` (cluster, namespace, workload kind/name) and lists existing policy-owned and finite manual-session packet/syscall sources. Each row states whether it can be saved (policy sources with recording saves enabled) and which live projections it supports. Rows contain only authorized source metadata, projection capabilities, state, history limits, hard end and actionable blockers. Opening a page does not arm a recorder or acquire a sample. No source means an explicit link to ordinary bounded collection or monitoring setup.

A viewer needs ordinary evidence-read entitlement under the source's retained audience (and `evidence:read` for OAuth clients), and the source's CollectionCeiling must allow live reads. Viewing acquires nothing, so no collection entitlement is required (ADR-335 D5). The source's executor authority and sensitive-observation grant must remain current. Creator or owner-manager status, an artifact share or a recording handle alone is insufficient; evidence-only share recipients cannot inspect live sources. No live share or new permission CRD is introduced. Operators may disable live views by scope/projection and bound them in existing deployment/CollectionCeiling configuration; opening an approved view needs no per-click human approval.

A source handle is an opaque, principal/client-bound reference to an exact workload/pod/source session and authorized projection; it has no wall-clock lifetime and is checked against current state when used. An open selects 1–16 distinct sources from one cluster/namespace/workload, within current authorized scope and live-view fanout limits. Different policies are permitted only with independent checks for every source; source audiences are not unioned to expand access. The selected projection must be supported and authorized for every source; reject mixed incompatible selection instead of dropping sources. No automatic inclusion of new pods, replacements, new sources or extra projections. Inventory may show existing stopped-source status, but opening a live watch requires a running Warming/Ready/Degraded source. Use ordinary saved evidence for historical/stopped sources.

### SC-LIV-001

Given an evidence reader of the workload, a share-only recipient and a same-named replacement pod, when they request live sources, then only the reader sees the sources, without holding collection rights. Opening the old handle cannot start or attach to a replacement recorder. Revoking the recorded executor's authority stops further live delivery even if the viewer's role remains. For a team policy this means its automation envelope, sponsoring groups' allows and grants; creator departure alone does not stop the source. A departed viewer loses live access independently.

## REQ-LIV-002 — Watch identity, filters and lifetime

`LiveWatch` is a principal/client-bound view, separate from Capture and SourceSession. Opening fixes exact source identities, projection and its delivery rate; it starts at acceptance, with no backfill. A watch stays open while a browser stream is connected or while MCP reads arrive at most 10 minutes apart (ADR-335 D9); after 10 minutes with neither it closes and releases subscriber resources. It also ends at Close, at the source's hard end and on loss of access. It has no other lifetime limit and never extends the source's hard end.

Opening authorizes the viewer and every source once. The decision is reused for at most `identity.AuthorityRecheckInterval` (30 s, ADR-311) and then re-evaluated against the hub's event-fed permission state. Revocation, deny, executor/grant loss, source end, namespace/pod replacement and retention cut-offs reach the watch as events (ADR-330 D1, D5) and end affected delivery immediately. No per-read or per-row authority check is made.

Typed display filters narrow only approved projected fields. They never edit acquisition BPF, Kubernetes peers, ring size, parser profile or recording grant. Support protocol/peer identity, literal remote IP in packet metadata, port/status/method and syscall name/error/PID metadata under field-specific operators and bounded literal values. No free-form CEL, regex, payload/body/header-name expressions, argv, dereference or container selector. Arbitrary dynamic peers remain acquisition configuration, not a live-view filter. Unknown/hidden fields cannot be used as a predicate oracle.

Filter changes and server-side Pause/Resume carry the expected view revision and an idempotency key, keep the original spend and increment the view revision. Changing a filter or resuming starts at the current source head with an explicit filterChanged/paused gap. A pause stops delivery for that viewer, not the source; the paused view still receives source-end and access-change events over its stream. Closing a tab closes its stream; the watch then closes after the 10-minute idle window unless the page reconnects. A disconnected view delivers no cached payload; reconnection under the same still-valid scope resumes from the current head with a gap. A multi-source watch may continue other originally selected authorized sources with explicit per-source status; forbidden source details are scrubbed on access loss.

### SC-LIV-002

Given two viewers with different filters, when one changes filters or pauses, then the other view and acquisition stay unchanged. Resuming does not backfill missed rows. New pod/source sessions are not substituted, and stale-revision commands cannot overwrite a newer view.

## REQ-LIV-003 — Bounded delivery, cursors and truthful gaps

The browser receives rows, gaps and state changes (pause, filter change, expiry, revocation, termination) over one Server-Sent Events stream per watch; reconnect uses `Last-Event-ID`. MCP uses bounded `watch_live(action=read)` batches: default 20 rows, maximum 100, and the existing 24 KiB target / 32 KiB structured / 64 KiB combined MCP limits. `wait_ms` is 0–30000; the server holds the read and returns on the first new row, state change or the wait limit. At most one outstanding read per watch. An empty batch is a timeout, not end-of-stream or proof that no activity occurred. Opening, changing or closing a watch is idempotent; retry cannot reset counters or mint duplicate subscribers.

Every row or batch carries watch revision, source session/filter generations, stable row identity `(sourceSessionId, sequence)`, observed and received times, clock uncertainty, coverage window, cumulative accounting, terminal state and explicit gap reasons. Sequence is monotonic within a source session; do not claim a global cross-node event order. HTTP observations may be emitted only after a bounded completed projection; absent request/response pairing and duration remain unknown. An MCP cursor or SSE event ID is bound to principal/client, source selection and view revision; it is not a grant. `next_cursor` is the continuation position, including on an empty batch, not evidence that more rows are available. Invalid or foreign cursors are rejected.

Retain at most 1 MiB of approved replay data per watch, inside shared workload/node/hub ceilings. Retrying the same cursor or event ID returns the same row identities and spends delivery bytes again; it does not advance acquisition. A position older than the replay buffer produces an explicit gap and resumes at the current head, never silent catch-up or historical rescanning.

Watch state lives in memory on the hub replica that serves it; source rows reach that replica as streams on the connector connection (ADR-330 D2). The watch's SQL row (REQ-LIV-005) names the serving replica. If that replica fails, the client's reconnect reaches another replica, which resumes from the current source head with a reconnect gap under the same watch row and spend. There is no exactly-once delivery or durable stream history. Clients deduplicate row IDs. Distinguish filtered-out rows, sensor loss, projection sampling, subscriber overflow, reconnect loss, filter change and unavailable/unknown coverage. Do not report missing counts as zero when unknown.

### SC-LIV-003

Given a slow reader or lost response during source rotation, when the client resumes, then duplicates are identifiable, gaps and unknown counts are explicit, and no replacement source fills the missing interval. A quiet fully observed interval is distinguishable from a disconnected source.

## REQ-LIV-004 — Protect collection and workload resources

Live viewing reserves separate bounded query/fanout memory, CPU/scan work, slots and transfer allowance. It cannot consume another workload's capacity or the reserved incident-save/upload allocation. Shipped defaults, which administrators may only narrow: 128 watches per installation, two per principal, four per workload, 16 subscriptions per node; 1 MiB replay and 64 KiB/s delivery per watch; per node 250 millicores of projection CPU, 50,000 examined events/s and 16 MiB/s examined bytes. Live views work with these defaults and no operator input. They are configuration limits, not measured overhead. Count every source subscription inside fanout limits and every transmitted retry against the delivery rate. Rolling hourly scan/delivery budgets per principal and workload are SQL counters; closing and opening a fresh watch does not reset them.

Build approved projections close to the existing source using independently bounded nonblocking readers. Reuse a qualified parser/projection only for identical source session/profile/authorization cohort; apply per-view filtering/authorization before delivery. No unbounded reparse or independent expensive dissection per viewer. Bound examined events/bytes and parser CPU, not just returned rows. Sensitive projection reads remain within the existing acquisition grant; there is no second observation or unapproved parser that expands source contents. CapabilityUnavailable is valid when an already retained representation cannot yield a qualified live projection.

Slow readers are sampled/dropped/disconnected with visible loss, never allowed to hold the collector, freeze raw rings or backpressure the workload. Incident snapshots have scheduling priority. Overload sheds live projections first; base recording remains governed by its own safety limits. Account the recorder baseline separately from incremental live-view cost and show both. Streaming needs no Kafka/Redis, per-row SQL write or object upload.

Operators see live-view load as Prometheus metrics on the hub and collectors: active watches and subscriptions (gauges), scanned/delivered bytes and rows, refusals, source-loss and reply-drop counts (counters), and pending-row buffer occupancy (gauge). Rates come from `rate()`. Labels are limited to cluster and projection; no node, workload, principal, source, filter, row or payload labels. Baseline recorder resources remain in the existing node/source metrics.

### SC-LIV-004

Given a syscall storm and many viewers, when live budgets are exhausted, then views show sampled/missing data or stop while recording and incident saves retain their allocated resources. Opening another tab, retrying or changing filters cannot reset principal/workload counters.

## REQ-LIV-005 — Privacy, transient data and saving

The first-release projections are `packetMetadata`, `httpHeaders` and `syscallMetadata`. They have fixed approved fields and minimization before leaving the collector. HTTP header values use the same configured allowlist/sanitizer as saved extracts; no body or generic arbitrary-header selector. Syscall metadata never reads user buffers, filenames or arguments. IPs, process identities and even approved headers may contain user-sensitive context: live does not mean nonsensitive. TLS plaintext, unsupported protocols or unsampled requests are not invented.

Displayed rows stay available while the watch is open, within the browser bound of 500 rows / 1 MiB and never past the source/content privacy deadline. The browser clears rows on watch close, logout and access loss. Disable caching, evidence-bearing telemetry, URL filters, localStorage, service-worker persistence and unbounded DOM growth. A reload restores only the watch configuration, never row data. In-flight buffers follow existing no-swap/no-secondary-core/log controls. MCP clients receive the same expiry and external-copy handling notice; Asservat cannot retract what an external client already copied.

The watch is an SQL row (ADR-330 D8): ID, principal/client, source selection, projection, filters, revision, serving replica, last activity and aggregate spend, written at open, update and close. Open, update and close each write one append-only audit row in the same transaction (ADR-330 D6). Row content never enters SQL, URLs, logs or audit.

Viewing is not durable evidence. **Save last N seconds** uses the [recording-save](recording-saves.md) preview and `request_capture`, checks the policy's save opt-in and shared quotas, and fixes t0 at save acceptance. It preserves the original source representation/window, not the display-filtered subset or displayed browser rows. The UI shows whether a selected row may have rotated out and never promises the exact row was saved before manifest verification. Pausing does not pin data. Manual-session sources point to their own capture outcomes; the policy-only save capability is not silently broadened. Sharing/raw inspection use committed artifacts and existing approval/retention rules. The live-view URI grants neither a share nor replay.

### SC-LIV-005

Given an approved HTTP row containing an allowed sensitive identifier, when the viewer closes or loses rights, then the browser clears the row and no row enters SQL/audit. Saving later reports its actual window and coverage rather than claiming preservation of a vanished displayed row. An evidence share grants no live access.

## REQ-LIV-006 — Human and agent experience

Add **Watch live** beside eligible recordings, policies, workloads and active manual captures. Source selection reports exact pods/actions/projections, authorized total versus selected count, capability gaps and incremental cost. Do not automatically attach simply by visiting a page. The live view shows Live/Paused/Disconnected/Closed, source hard end, last received/observed times, loss/lag, capped row count, source state, filter-generation changes and explicit **Pause view**, **Resume from now**, **Close view**, **Save last N seconds** and **Connect agent** actions. Follow-tail does not steal focus; inspecting a row freezes selection, not source history. Fixed metadata filters and click-to-filter chips keep narrowing easy. Unsupported details offer saved-artifact inspection, not a false plaintext preview.

MCP uses `list_recordings` for inventory and one tool, `watch_live(action=open|read|update|close)`, for the watch. Agents consume bounded batches, optionally stopping when a typed match appears; no unbounded tool results or model-context flooding. open/update/close mutate only the viewer's watch and do not need evidence:capture. All need evidence:read plus the viewer rights of REQ-LIV-001. Results explain that rows are transient/untrusted and a save is a separate explicit operation. Browser and MCP share the same row, gap, state and bound schemas; only the transport differs (SSE versus long-poll batches). No privileged CLI, raw websocket or payload-bearing notification channel is added.

### SC-LIV-006

Given an evidence reader or authorized agent, when they watch packet/HTTP and syscall metadata, then they can narrow, pause/resume, detect loss, close and explicitly save eligible sources without confusing display filters with acquisition filters. The browser and agent see equivalent bounds and eligibility; an agent that reasons for several minutes between reads keeps its watch; recipients have no live navigation.
